# Steel Plate Fabrication and Assembly

> **Domain:** ship | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Plate fabrication turns flat steel plate into the shaped shells and frames
of a hull. The key principle is that the edges are the problem: the mill's
tolerance, the heat number, and the distortion from cutting and welding
all accumulate at the joints, so the control of the edge and the marking
of the parts decide whether the hull fits together or does not. Marking and
cutting well is worth more than any amount of grinding afterwards.

## Prerequisites

- The hull lines plan and the offsets
- A nest or cutting list and the plate thickness schedule
- The structural arrangement, including the frame and girder layout
- Steel identification and the mill certificates
- Hot work safety, fire watch, and the gas handling rules

## Materials & Tools

- Steel plate with mill certificates showing the grade and the heat
- Templates, or nesting software and a marking-out table
- Oxy-fuel and plasma cutting sets with correct regulators and hoses
- Plate rolls, a press brake, and a bevelling machine or grinder
- Tape, straightedge, templates, chalk, and a centre punch
- Rivets and a riveting gun, or a welding set with the qualified procedure
- Clamps, wedges, strongbacks, and a jig table with a grid
- Fire blankets, a fire watch kit, and a portable gas detector

## Procedure

### Mark and cut

1. Check the plate's heat number against the certificate, verify the
   thickness with a caliper, and reject plate with heavy scale or serious
   surface defects.
2. Mark the parts from the templates and the nest, adding the allowance
   for the edge that the flame will melt away: about 1 to 3 mm per cut
   edge.
3. Mark the part number, the grade, and the heat number on every piece,
   so that the traceability survives the cutting and the years.
4. Cut the parts along the marks and keep the edge clean and square; an
   out-of-square edge shows up later as a mismatch at the butt.

### Form the plate

1. Choose the method: cold rolling for a radius of more than about four
   times the thickness, line heating for tighter radii in thick plate,
   and press forming for straight flanges and edge folds.
2. Roll with the grain where the direction matters, and respect the
   material: for mild steel the minimum bend radius is about 1.5 to 2
   times the thickness.
3. Overbend slightly for a tight radius on thick plate, by 1 to 2
   degrees, because the plate will spring back.
4. Check the developed shape against the template on the jig table, and
   match it at three points before accepting the part.

### Fit and assemble

1. Set the block up on the jig from the datum lines and hold the parts
   with clamps and strongbacks; never force a plate to fit.
2. Tack the parts with the correct root gap, 1 to 3 mm for a butt joint,
   and check the diagonals of the block: they should agree within 1
   percent of the diagonal.
3. Weld in the sequence the procedure specifies, butting the joints that
   take the most shrinkage first.
4. Measure the block's offsets against the moulded line before it leaves
   the jig, and take out any twist while the block is still on the table.

## Safety Notes

- Hot work: keep a fire watch on the vessel and in the yard for at least
  60 minutes after the last arc, and check the void spaces, which are
  easy to miss because nobody can see into them.
- Keep the oxygen and the fuel gas cylinders upright, capped, and chained,
  and separate them by at least 1.5 m from each other and from any flame.
- The plate rolls and the press crush: keep hands out of the roll gap,
  lock out the drive, and never reach in while it turns.
- Hot metal and sharp edges: a plate edge at 200 degrees Celsius burns
  through a cotton shirt, so use burn-proof gloves, sleeves, and eye
  protection.
- Weld fume in a confined block is a serious exposure: ventilate and use
  local extraction at the source.
- A plate of 25 mm steel weighs about 200 kg per square metre, so a big
  part falling will crush, and the edges will cut.
- Test the atmosphere before entering a double bottom or a tank, and never
  enter on a torch alone; a torch alone does not make a space safe.
