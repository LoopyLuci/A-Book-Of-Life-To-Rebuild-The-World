# Safe Household Water Storage

> **Domain:** water | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** yellow

## Overview

Storing water turns a lucky find into a reliable supply. The container matters
as much as the source: a clean, covered, food-grade tank keeps water drinkable,
while an open drum or a used chemical barrel can make safe water unsafe within
days. Plan for at least 4 L per person per day for survival and 15-20 L for
drinking, cooking, and basic hygiene.

## Prerequisites

- A treated or protected water source
- A shaded, level site that will not flood

## Materials & Tools

- Food-grade HDPE, stainless steel, or sealed concrete tank
- Fitted lid and insect screen
- Tap or spigot mounted above the bottom
- Sediment filter or clean cloth for filling
- Chlorine test strips or a pool-test kit
- Pallet or raised base
- Labels and a logbook

## Procedure

### 1. Choose and prepare the container

1. Use containers made for drinking water. Avoid barrels that held fuel,
   pesticides, or unknown chemicals; plastic can absorb and release them.
2. Clean with water and a mild detergent, rinse thoroughly, then disinfect with
   a chlorine solution and rinse again.
3. Fit a tight lid and a screened vent. Cut no openings that let hands or
   insects into the stored water.

### 2. Site and install

1. Place the tank on a level, compacted base that can carry the full weight.
2. Keep it out of direct sun; heat encourages bacterial growth and algae.
3. Fit the tap above the sludge layer and slope the base toward a drain.
4. Route overflow away from the foundation and away from gardens where
   children play.

### 3. Fill and treat

1. Fill with the cleanest water available and filter out visible sediment.
2. For clear water, add the locally recommended chlorine dose, mix, and wait
   the required contact time, usually 30 minutes.
3. Test residual chlorine; if there is no faint chlorine smell, repeat the dose
   and retest. Do not over-chlorinate.
4. Cover immediately and record the source and date.

### 4. Rotate and monitor

1. Use older water first and refill before the tank runs low.
2. Inspect monthly for algae, sediment, a loose lid, or a leaking tap.
3. Clean the tank every 6-12 months or when sediment reaches the tap level.
4. If water becomes cloudy or smells musty, treat it again and clean the tank.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Stored water can grow bacteria and algae. Treat drinking
  water and keep containers covered.
- Never store water in containers that held leaded fuel, pesticides, or
  industrial chemicals.
- Open tanks are a drowning hazard for children. Keep them covered and fenced.
- A 1,000 L tank weighs about 1 tonne; check the floor or stand before filling.
