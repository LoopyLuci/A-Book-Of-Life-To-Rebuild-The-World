# Well Maintenance

> **Domain:** water | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

A well fails slowly before it fails completely. Falling yield, dirty water,
a loose cap, or a pump that needs priming are warnings that the sanitary seal,
pump, or gravel pack is compromised. Routine service protects the source and
avoids the dangerous work of pulling a pump or entering a dug well.

## Prerequisites

- Well construction knowledge
- Pump and pipe repair skills
- Water quality testing

## Materials & Tools

- Spare pump seals, gaskets, and rope
- Chlorine bleach with known concentration
- pH test kit and chlorine test strips
- Clean buckets, brushes, and disposable rags
- Wrench set, pipe wrench, and pump puller
- Strong rope and a tripod or winch
- Well cap, screened vent, and concrete sealant
- Water level meter or weighted rope

## Procedure

### 1. Inspect the wellhead

1. Check that the cap is secure, the vent is screened, and the concrete apron
   slopes away from the casing.
2. Look for cracks, rodent burrows, standing water, and evidence of flooding.
3. Confirm that no pesticide, fuel, or animal waste is stored within 30 m
   uphill of the well.
4. Record static water level and pump runtime before making changes.

### 2. Service the pump

1. Follow the pump manufacturer's instructions; isolate power and lock out
   controls before touching an electric pump.
2. Pull a hand pump or submersible only with a tripod, winch, and trained
   helpers; never lift by the pipe alone.
3. Replace worn seals, gaskets, and check valves; clean or replace the intake
   screen.
4. Reinstall with a new safety rope and torque fittings to the maker's values.

### 3. Disinfect the well

1. Calculate the water volume in the casing and add enough chlorine to reach
   about 50-100 mg/L, following local guidance.
2. Circulate the chlorinated water through the pump and distribution lines.
3. Leave it for at least 12-24 hours, then flush until chlorine is below the
   drinking-water limit.
4. Test for chlorine residual and bacteria before returning the well to service.

### 4. Monitor and protect

1. Measure yield and drawdown monthly; a sudden drop may mean a failing pump or
   a falling aquifer.
2. Record rainfall, water level, and any nearby construction or mining.
3. Re-test water after floods, earthquakes, or any change in taste or clarity.
4. Keep a spare seal kit and a written service log at the well.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Wells can contain lethal gases and low oxygen. Never enter a
  dug well without confined-space training, gas testing, and a rescue plan.
- Electric pumps and wet ground are a shock hazard; isolate power before work.
- Chlorine is corrosive and can release dangerous gas when mixed with acids.
- Heavy pump assemblies can crush hands and feet; use a tripod and a spotter.
