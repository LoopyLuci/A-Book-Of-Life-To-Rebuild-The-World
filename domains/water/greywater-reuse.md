# Greywater Reuse

> **Domain:** water | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

## Overview

Greywater is water from baths, showers, hand-washing, and laundry. It can carry
50-80% of household wastewater and still contains enough nutrients to irrigate
trees, shrubs, and some crops. The safe approach is to separate it from toilet
water, limit chemicals, and apply it below the soil surface without storing it.

## Prerequisites

- A separate drain for greywater
- A garden or landscape area that can use the water
- Local rules on reuse and setbacks

## Materials & Tools

- Diverter valve and greywater-safe pipe
- Mulch basin or subsurface irrigation line
- Gravel and filter screen
- Shovel and spirit level
- Gloves and eye protection
- Low-salt, borax-free soap

## Procedure

### 1. Plan the system

1. Check local health rules and keep setbacks from wells, streams, and
   foundations.
2. Estimate flow: a low-use household produces roughly 100-150 L per day.
3. Match the flow to the planting area; do not oversaturate the soil.
4. Choose a simple gravity system with a diverter so greywater can go to the
   sewer or septic system when needed.

### 2. Separate and pre-filter

1. Route bath, shower, sink, and laundry water to the diverter.
2. Keep toilet water, kitchen grease, and dishwasher water out of the system.
3. Fit a coarse screen to catch hair and lint; clean it weekly.
4. Avoid chlorine bleach, borax, and strong disinfectants that harm soil life.

### 3. Apply below the surface

1. Run pipe to a mulch basin around a tree or shrub, or to subsurface drip line.
2. Cover the outlet with mulch to limit contact, odour, and mosquitoes.
3. Alternate application points so the soil can recover.
4. Do not irrigate root crops, leafy greens, or anything eaten raw.

### 4. Monitor and maintain

1. Check for pooling, odour, or mosquito larvae; fix blockages promptly.
2. Watch for salt buildup, especially in arid soils; leach with clean water.
3. Keep a record of soaps and cleaning products used.
4. Stop reuse and repair the system if any household member has an infectious
   gastrointestinal illness.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Greywater contains pathogens. Never store it for more than
  24 hours and never drink it.
- Apply below ground and keep children and pets away from outlets.
- Do not use greywater on edible roots or leaves eaten raw.
- Avoid contamination of wells, springs, and surface water; maintain setbacks.
