# Rainwater Harvesting

> **Domain:** water | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

A rainwater system collects runoff from a clean roof, diverts the first dirty
water, screens out leaves, and stores the rest in a covered tank. It is most
useful where rain is seasonal, groundwater is deep, or piped supply is
unreliable. Catchment is simple arithmetic: 1 mm of rain on 1 m² yields about
1 L, and a well-designed system captures 60-90% of that after losses.

## Prerequisites

- Basic roofing and gutter work
- A level tank site or strong stand

## Materials & Tools

- Roof catchment area, preferably metal, tile, or coated concrete
- Gutters, downpipes, and brackets
- First-flush diverter and leaf screen
- Food-grade storage tank with a tight lid
- Overflow pipe directed away from foundations
- Filter sand and gravel
- Chlorine or another approved disinfectant

## Procedure

### 1. Calculate supply and demand

1. Measure the roof footprint in square metres.
2. Multiply by annual rainfall in millimetres and by 0.6-0.9 for losses.
3. Compare with household demand: at least 15-20 L per person per day for
   drinking, cooking, and basic hygiene.
4. Size the tank for the longest dry spell, not just the average storm.

### 2. Prepare the catchment

1. Remove lead paint, asbestos, and heavy bird droppings from the roof.
2. Slope gutters toward the downpipe at roughly 3-5 mm per metre.
3. Fit a leaf screen at the tank inlet and a first-flush diverter that discards
   the first 1-2 mm of rain.
4. Keep trees from overhanging the roof where possible.

### 3. Install the tank

1. Level and compact the base; use a concrete pad or compacted gravel.
2. Raise the tank on a stable stand if a tap is fitted.
3. Connect the inlet, overflow, and a bottom drain for cleaning.
4. Seal all openings with insect mesh; never leave an open water surface.

### 4. Treat and maintain

1. Inspect and clean gutters before the rainy season.
2. Remove sediment from the tank every 6-12 months.
3. Treat drinking water by boiling, filtration plus disinfection, or
   chlorination; test the residual chlorine.
4. Label the tank and record the date of cleaning.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Roof runoff can carry pathogens, heavy metals, and
  pesticides. Never drink untreated rainwater without assessing the roof and
  treating the water.
- Large tanks are a drowning risk. Keep lids fastened and fence open cisterns.
- A full tank is heavy: 1,000 L weighs about 1 tonne. Build for the full load.
- Do not connect rainwater to a municipal supply without a licensed
  backflow preventer.
