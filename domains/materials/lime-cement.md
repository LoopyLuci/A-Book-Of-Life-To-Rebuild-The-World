# Lime & Cement

## Overview

Lime and cement are binders for masonry and concrete. Lime comes from
limestone (CaCO₃) burned to quicklime (CaO). Cement adds silica and alumina.

- [Brick Making](brick-making.md)
- [Charcoal Production](charcoal-production.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Limestone or chalk
- Kiln
- Water
- Sand, gravel (aggregate)
- Pozzolan (volcanic ash or fired clay)

## Procedure

### Lime Burning

1. Crush limestone to 5 cm pieces.
2. Fill kiln; fire to 900–1,000°C for 12–24 hours.
3. Quicklime (CaO) results — highly caustic.
4. Store in sealed containers away from moisture.

### Slaking

1. Slowly add water to quicklime (violent exotherm).
2. Result is slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂) — putty or powder.
3. Let mature 1–4 weeks before use.

### Lime Mortar

1. Mix 1 part lime putty to 3 parts sand.
2. Add water to workable consistency.
3. Use within 2 hours.
4. Cures by carbonation (absorbs CO₂) — slow, months.

### Hydraulic Lime

1. Limestone with 10–20% clay fires to hydraulic lime.
2. Sets underwater — useful for foundations and cisterns.

### Roman Cement (Pozzolanic)

1. Mix slaked lime with pozzolan (volcanic ash) 2:1.
2. Add sand and aggregate.
3. Sets hard underwater — used in aqueducts and harbors.

### Portland Cement (Modern)

1. Burn limestone + clay at 1,450°C to clinker.
2. Grind clinker with 5% gypsum to powder.
3. Mix with sand, gravel, water (1:2:3 by volume) for concrete.
4. Cure 28 days for full strength.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Quicklime burns skin and eyes on contact; slaking releases
  boiling water. Wear full PPE.
- Cement dust causes silicosis and chemical burns — respirator, gloves.
- Never add water to cement in a confined space (heat and steam).
