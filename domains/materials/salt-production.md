# Salt Production and Iodization

> **Domain:** materials | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

## Overview

Salt preserves food, supports health, and trades well. Seawater contains about
3.5% salt, so 1,000 L can yield roughly 20-30 kg of usable salt after losses.
Solar evaporation is cheapest in hot, dry, windy places; boiling works in wet
climates but consumes fuel. Iodization prevents goitre and cretinism, so it is
part of making salt safe and useful.

## Prerequisites

- Access to clean seawater, brine springs, or rock salt
- A flat, sun-exposed site or fuel for boiling

## Materials & Tools

- Clean seawater or brine
- Shallow evaporation pans lined with clay or concrete
- Filter cloth
- Fuel and a boiling vessel for wet climates
- Iodine or iodized salt for fortification
- Sealed storage containers

## Procedure

### 1. Source and filter

1. Collect seawater away from sewage outfalls, harbours, and industrial
   discharges.
2. Let the water settle, then filter through cloth to remove sand and debris.
3. Concentrate brine in a pre-evaporation pan before the final crystallizer.
4. Do not use lead pipes or galvanized containers for acidic brine.

### 2. Evaporate

1. Fill shallow pans and expose them to sun and wind.
2. Stir occasionally and top up with fresh brine as it evaporates.
3. Remove scum and dirt as they collect.
4. In wet weather, boil brine in a clean vessel, skimming the surface.

### 3. Harvest and wash

1. Scrape crystals before the brine dries completely; bitter salts remain in
   the liquid.
2. Drain the salt in a cloth-lined basket.
3. Wash briefly with clean saturated brine, not fresh water, to remove
   impurities without dissolving the product.
4. Dry in the sun or a warm, ventilated place.

### 4. Iodize and store

1. Add iodine at the locally recommended rate, or blend with iodized salt.
2. Mix thoroughly and test if a kit is available.
3. Pack in dry, sealed containers; moisture makes salt cake and corrodes metal.
4. Label the batch and keep it away from fuel, pesticides, and animals.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Boiling brine causes severe burns. Use long handles,
  gloves, and eye protection.
- Contaminated seawater can produce salt laced with heavy metals or pathogens.
- Salt production is dehydrating work; drink water and rest in shade.
- Do not rely on uniodized salt alone in regions where iodine deficiency is
  common.
