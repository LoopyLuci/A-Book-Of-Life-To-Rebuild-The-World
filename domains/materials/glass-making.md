# Glass Making

## Overview

Glass is made from silica (sand), soda ash, and lime, melted at 1,400°C.
It is essential for windows, lenses, containers, and labware.

## Prerequisites

- [Charcoal Production](charcoal-production.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Silica sand (white, clean)
- Soda ash (from burnt kelp or natron)
- Lime (from limestone)
- Crucible (clay)
- Blowpipe
- Annealing oven

## Procedure

### Glass Batch

1. Mix by weight: 70% silica, 15% soda ash, 10% lime, 5% cullet (recycled glass).
2. Grind to powder; mix thoroughly.

### Melting

1. Heat crucible in furnace to 1,400°C.
2. Add batch slowly; it will foam as CO₂ escapes.
3. Skim off impurities.
4. Hold at 1,200°C for working.

### Blowing

1. Dip blowpipe in molten glass; gather a gob.
2. Roll on a marver (metal plate) to shape.
3. Blow gently through the pipe; rotate continuously.
4. Shape with jacks (tongs) and a wooden paddle.
5. Transfer to a punty rod for the open end.

### Annealing

1. Place finished glass in an oven at 500°C.
2. Cool slowly (1°C/min) to room temperature.
3. Rapid cooling causes shattering.

### Flat Glass (Cylinder Method)

1. Blow a large cylinder.
2. Cut off ends; slit lengthwise.
3. Reheat and flatten in an oven.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Molten glass causes severe burns and sticks to skin.
- Furnace gases (CO, SO₂) are toxic — ventilate.
- Silica dust causes silicosis — wear respirator when mixing batch.
- Never quench hot glass in water.
