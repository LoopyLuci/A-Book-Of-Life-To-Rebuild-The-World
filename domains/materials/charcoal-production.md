# Charcoal Production

> **Domain:** materials | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Charcoal burns hotter and cleaner than wood and is essential for smelting
and blacksmithing. It is made by pyrolysis — burning wood with limited oxygen.

## Prerequisites

- [Making Fire](../survival/making-fire.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Hardwood (oak, hickory, mesquite)
- Earth or sand
- Shovel
- Water (for quenching)

## Procedure

### Earth Kiln Method

1. Clear a 2 m circle; level the ground.
2. Stack hardwood in a dome, leaving a central chimney.
3. Cover the pile with leaves, then earth, leaving the chimney open.
4. Light the chimney.
5. When smoke turns from white to blue, close the chimney.
6. Tend for 3–7 days, patching cracks as they appear.
7. When smoke stops, wait 24 hours; then open and quench with water.

### Retort Method (Higher Yield)

1. Place wood in a sealed metal drum.
2. Heat from outside; volatile gases escape via a pipe.
3. Those gases can fuel the fire — self-sustaining once started.
4. Cool before opening.

### Yield

- Earth kiln: 10–20% by weight
- Retort: 25–35%

### Uses

- Smelting iron, copper
- Blacksmithing forges
- Water filtration (activated charcoal)
- Gunpowder (see [Chemistry](chemistry.md))
- Medicinal (charcoal slurry for poisoning)

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Carbon monoxide from smoldering charcoal kills without
  warning. Work outdoors.
- Never seal a drum while hot — pressure builds.
- Quench thoroughly; buried embers can reignite days later.
