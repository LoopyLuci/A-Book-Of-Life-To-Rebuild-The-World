# Adhesives

> **Domain:** materials | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

## Overview

Simple adhesives can be made from starch, hide, milk protein, and lime. Each
has a different strength, water resistance, and working time. The joint usually
fails because the surfaces are dirty, the fit is poor, or the glue is too thick;
surface preparation matters more than the recipe.

## Prerequisites

- Measuring by weight
- Basic heating and woodworking skills

## Materials & Tools

- Wheat or cassava starch
- Hide glue granules or sheets
- Casein powder or skim milk
- Slaked lime or borax as required by the recipe
- Clean water
- Sandpaper and a stiff brush
- Heat source and double boiler
- Scales, cups, and a stirring stick
- Clamps and scrap wood for test joints

## Procedure

### 1. Prepare the surfaces

1. Remove dust, oil, old glue, and loose fibres.
2. Roughen smooth surfaces slightly and dry them.
3. Test the fit; a gap thicker than about 0.2 mm weakens most natural glues.
4. Warm the room and the parts; cold surfaces shorten working time.

### 2. Make starch paste

1. Mix one part starch with enough cold water to make a smooth slurry.
2. Add about five parts hot water while stirring, then heat gently until the
   mixture turns clear and thick.
3. Stir out lumps and thin with water if needed; the paste should spread like
   thick cream.
4. Use while warm; starch paste has a short working life and is not waterproof.

### 3. Make hide glue

1. Soak hide glue in water until soft, then heat it gently in a double boiler.
2. Do not boil; overheating degrades the protein and weakens the joint.
3. Adjust water to reach a syrupy consistency and skim any foam.
4. Apply warm, clamp firmly, and leave overnight.

### 4. Make casein glue

1. Follow a tested recipe that combines casein, water, and a small amount of
   alkali such as slaked lime or borax.
2. Mix to a smooth paste and let it stand briefly before use.
3. Apply a thin even coat, clamp, and keep the joint dry while it cures.
4. Test on scrap before using on a finished piece.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Hot glue and caustic lime can cause burns. Wear gloves and
  eye protection.
- Hide glue and starch can grow mould; keep containers clean and discard spoiled
  batches.
- Use casein in a ventilated area and avoid inhaling powder.
- Do not use unknown adhesives on food containers, water pipes, or medical
  items.
