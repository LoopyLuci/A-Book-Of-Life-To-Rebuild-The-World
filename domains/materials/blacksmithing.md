## Overview

Blacksmithing shapes wrought iron and steel by heating and hammering. It
produces tools, weapons, hardware, and fasteners.

## Prerequisites

- [Charcoal Production](charcoal-production.md)
- [Metal Smelting](metal-smelting.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Forge (clay or steel)
- Anvil (or dense stone)
- Hammer (2–4 kg)
- Tongs
- Quench tank (oil or water)
- Charcoal

## Procedure

### Setting Up the Forge

1. Build a clay hearth with a tuyere at the center.
2. Connect bellows or blower.
3. Fill with charcoal; light from the top.
4. Air blast raises temp to 1,200–1,400°C.

### Basic Forging

1. Heat metal to bright orange-yellow (1,100°C).
2. Remove; hammer immediately.
3. Reheat as it turns cherry red (below 800°C).
4. Shape by drawing, upsetting, bending, or punching.

### Drawing Out

1. Heat a section.
2. Hammer on the anvil's horn or face, striking perpendicular to length.
3. Rotate 90° between blows to keep square.

### Upsetting

1. Heat the end.
2. Strike downward onto the anvil; thickness increases, length shortens.

### Welding (Forge Weld)

1. Heat both pieces to white-yellow (1,300°C).
2. Sprinkle borax as flux.
3. Overlap; hammer rapidly. The pieces fuse.

### Heat Treatment

1. **Annealing:** heat to red, cool slowly in ash.
2. **Normalizing:** heat to red, air-cool.
3. **Hardening:** heat to red, quench in oil or water.
4. **Tempering:** reheat to 200–300°C (straw to purple color), then cool.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Burns, eye injuries, and hearing damage are common.
- Wear leather apron, eye protection, ear protection.
- Never quench in water without knowing the steel — cracking risk.
- Keep water away from the forge unless intentional.
- Ventilate for CO.
