# Mills

> **Domain:** materials | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources

## Overview

Mills grind grain, press oil, saw wood, and full cloth. They are powered by
water, wind, or animals. Mills are force multipliers for any community.

## Prerequisites

- [Simple Tools](../survival/simple-tools.md)
- [Rope & Cordage](../survival/rope-cordage.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Millstones (hard granite or basalt)
- Wood for frame, gears, and wheel
- Metal bearings and shafts (see [Blacksmithing](blacksmithing.md))
- Water source or wind exposure

## Procedure

### Water Mill (Undershot)

1. Site on a stream with year-round flow.
2. Build a wheel 2–4 m diameter with paddles.
3. Mount on a horizontal axle; support with wooden or metal bearings.
4. Couple to millstone via gears (see [Blacksmithing](blacksmithing.md)).
5. Build a sluice to direct water onto the paddles.

### Water Mill (Overshot — More Efficient)

1. Build a flume to carry water over the top of the wheel.
2. Wheel has buckets instead of paddles.
3. Water weight drives the wheel downward.

### Windmill

1. Tower 6–12 m tall with a rotating top (cap).
2. Sails: 4 arms, 5–8 m each, covered with cloth or slats.
3. Tail pole or fantail keeps sails facing the wind.
4. Gearing drives millstone or pump.

### Animal Mill

1. Central post; horizontal beam.
2. Animal (ox, horse) walks in a circle.
3. Beam turns a vertical shaft connected to the millstone.

### Millstone Dressing

1. Cut grooves in a pattern (harps, quarter dress) to shear grain.
2. Sharpen every 100 hours of use.
3. Gap between stones: 0.3–0.6 mm for flour.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Rotating shafts and gears cause entanglement. Guard them.
- Mill dust is explosive — ventilate.
- Millstones can shatter; never over-speed.
- Hearing protection near working mills.
