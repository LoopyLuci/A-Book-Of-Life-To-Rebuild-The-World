# Leather Tanning

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources

## Overview

Tanning stabilizes animal hide against rot. Methods: vegetable (bark),
brain, alum, and chrome. Vegetable is the safest and most traditional.

- [Raising Livestock](../agriculture/raising-livestock.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Fresh hide
- Bark (oak, chestnut, mimosa — high tannin)
- Lime (for dehairing)
- Fleshing knife
- Tanning vat
- Wooden beam

## Procedure

### Fleshing

1. Soak the hide in water 1–2 days.
2. Lay over a beam; scrape flesh and fat with a dull knife.
3. Remove hair by soaking in lime water (pH 12) 1–2 weeks.
4. Scrape hair and epidermis.

### Deliming & Bating

1. Rinse in water.
2. Soak in a weak acid (vinegar or bran water) 1 day to neutralize lime.
3. Bate (soften) in a warm manure or enzyme solution 1–3 days.

### Vegetable Tanning

1. Build a series of vats with increasing tannin concentration.
2. Start with weak "spent" tan liquor (1:10 bark to water).
3. Move the hide through stronger liquors over 4–12 weeks.
4. Finish in a strong tan pit 1–4 weeks.
5. When fully tanned (uniform brown throughout), remove.

### Brain Tanning (Native American Method)

1. Every animal has enough brain to tan its own hide.
2. Mash brain with water; work into the hide.
3. Stretch and dry; work with hands and a rope to soften.

### Finishing

1. Rinse.
2. Oil with tallow or fish oil.
3. Stretch and dry slowly.
4. Work with a slicker and stake to soften.
5. Smoke over a smoldering fire for color and water resistance.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Lime burns skin; wear gloves and eye protection.
- Raw hides carry zoonotic diseases (anthrax, brucellosis) — handle with care.
- Tanning solutions smell; work outdoors, away from dwellings.
