## Overview

Chemistry underpins soap, medicine, metallurgy, and agriculture. This entry
covers the foundational reactions and safety rules needed to produce acids,
bases, salts, and simple organic compounds.

## Prerequisites

- [Metal Smelting](metal-smelting.md)
- [Charcoal Production](charcoal-production.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Glass vessels (see [Glass Making](glass-making.md))
- Heat source (charcoal forge)
- Acids: vinegar (acetic), citric (lemon), sulfuric (from sulfide ores)
- Bases: lime (CaO), wood ash (K₂CO₃), ammonia (from urine)
- Litmus or red cabbage indicator

## Procedure

### Safety First

1. Always add acid to water, never water to acid.
2. Wear eye protection and gloves.
3. Work outdoors or under a fume hood.
4. Have a bucket of water and baking soda within reach.

### Making Sulfuric Acid (Lead Chamber)

1. Burn sulfur in a lead-lined chamber with moist air.
2. SO₂ + O₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₄.
3. Collect the acid at the chamber base.
4. Concentrate by boiling off water.

### Making Nitric Acid (Saltpeter + Sulfuric)

1. Mix saltpeter (KNO₃) with sulfuric acid in a glass retort.
2. Heat gently; distill nitric acid into a cooled receiver.
3. Store in dark glass.

### Making Hydrochloric Acid (Salt + Sulfuric)

1. Mix table salt with sulfuric acid.
2. Collect HCl gas in water.
3. Store in glass.

### Making Lye (Potassium Hydroxide)

1. Leach hardwood ash with rainwater.
2. Evaporate to concentrate.
3. Test with a fresh egg — it floats when strong enough.

### Making Soap (Saponification)

See [Soap Making](../survival/soap-making.md).

### Making Gunpowder

1. Mix 75% potassium nitrate, 15% charcoal, 10% sulfur by weight.
2. Mill to a fine powder.
3. **Red hazard:** Static sparks ignite powder. Work with wooden tools, ground yourself.

### pH Testing (Red Cabbage Indicator)

1. Boil red cabbage; save the purple liquid.
2. Add a drop of test solution.
3. Red = acid, green = base, purple = neutral.

### Common Reactions

- **Neutralization:** acid + base → salt + water.
- **Oxidation:** metal + oxygen → oxide.
- **Reduction:** oxide + carbon → metal + CO₂.
- **Combustion:** fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Concentrated acids and bases destroy tissue on contact.
- **Red hazard:** Many reactions produce toxic or explosive gases.
- Never taste or smell chemicals directly.
- Never store acids in metal containers.
- Ventilate always.
