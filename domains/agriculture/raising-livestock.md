# Raising Livestock

> **Domain:** agriculture | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Livestock convert grass and waste into protein, fat, leather, and fertilizer.
Start small — chickens and rabbits first, then goats or pigs, then cattle.

- [Composting](composting.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Fencing
- Feed (grain, hay, forage)
- Water source
- Shelter
- Veterinary basics (see [Health](../health/README.md))

## Procedure

### Chickens

1. Coop: 0.4 m² per bird, roosts 30 cm off ground.
2. Feed: 100 g layer pellets per bird per day + forage.
3. Water: 250 mL per bird per day.
4. Eggs: 1 per hen per day in peak season.
5. Slaughter at 18–24 months for stewing hens.

### Rabbits

1. Hutch: 0.5 m² per adult.
2. Feed: hay ad libitum, 100 g pellets, greens.
3. Breeding: 1 buck per 10 does; gestation 30 days.
4. Yield: 4–8 kg meat per year per doe.

### Goats (Dairy)

1. Shelter: 2 m² per goat, dry bedding.
2. Feed: browse + 1 kg hay + 0.5 kg grain per day.
3. Milk: 2–4 L per day, twice daily.
4. Breeding: seasonal; gestation 150 days.

### Pigs

1. Pen: 10 m² per pig.
2. Feed: kitchen waste, grain, forage.
3. Farrowing: 1 litter per year, 8–12 piglets.
4. Slaughter at 6–8 months (100 kg).

### Cattle

1. Pasture: 0.4–1 ha per cow.
2. Feed: grass/hay + minerals.
3. Milk: 15–30 L/day for dairy breeds.
4. Breeding: 1 bull per 30 cows; gestation 280 days.

### Health Basics

- Quarantine new animals 2 weeks.
- Vaccinate per local disease profile.
- Deworm quarterly.
- Isolate sick animals immediately.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Zoonotic diseases (brucellosis, rabies, salmonella)
  transmit from animals.
- Always wash hands after handling.
- Bulls and boars can be aggressive; never turn your back.
- Slaughter humanely; stun before bleeding.
