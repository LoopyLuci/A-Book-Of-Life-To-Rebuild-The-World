# Seed Saving

> **Domain:** agriculture | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** green

## Overview

Saved seed preserves genetic diversity and independence. Open-pollinated
(heirloom) varieties breed true; hybrids do not.

## Prerequisites

- [Soil Testing](soil-testing.md)
- [Crop Rotation](crop-rotation.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Healthy plants (select best specimens)
- Cloth bags
- Glass jars
- Silica desiccant

## Procedure

### Self-Pollinating (Tomato, Bean, Pea, Lettuce)

1. Let fruits mature fully on the plant.
2. For tomatoes: ferment seeds in water 3 days to remove gel.
3. Rinse, dry on a plate (not paper towel).
4. Store in a cool, dark, dry place.

### Cross-Pollinating (Corn, Squash, Brassicas)

1. Isolate varieties by 400 m or use hand-pollination.
2. Bag unopened flowers; hand-pollinate with a brush.
3. Re-bag until fruit sets.

### Biennials (Carrot, Onion, Cabbage)

1. Overwinter roots/bulbs.
2. Replant in spring.
3. Harvest seed heads when dry.

### Storage

1. Dry seeds to <10% moisture (they snap, not bend).
2. Store in glass jars with desiccant.
3. Label: species, variety, year, source.
4. Cool (5–10°C), dark, dry. Viability: 3–10 years.

## Safety Notes

- Do not save seed from diseased plants.
- Do not save seed from F1 hybrids.
- Label meticulously — misidentified seed is worse than no seed.
