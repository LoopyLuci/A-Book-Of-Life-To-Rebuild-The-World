# Water Governance

> **Domain:** society | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

A water point lasts when a community agrees who may use it, who maintains it,
who pays, and how disputes are settled. Governance is not a document on a shelf;
it is a monthly meeting, a visible account, and a maintenance fund. Without
those, pumps break and the poorest households lose access first.

## Prerequisites

- A functioning water source or storage system
- A trusted group willing to serve on a committee

## Materials & Tools

- Written rules and a map of the system
- Membership and user register
- Cash book, receipts, and a lockbox
- Maintenance log and spare-parts list
- Notice board
- Meeting space and minutes book

## Procedure

### 1. Define the water system and users

1. Map the source, storage, pipes, taps, and private connections.
2. List households, institutions, livestock, and businesses that use the system.
3. Identify who owns the land and who holds customary or legal water rights.
4. Agree on the boundaries of the service area.

### 2. Set rules and responsibilities

1. Elect a committee with clear terms, including women and minority users.
2. Write rules for access, queueing, livestock, gardening, and private
   connections.
3. Assign maintenance tasks and name who can authorize repairs.
4. Set penalties for damage, theft, and pollution that are fair and enforceable.

### 3. Set a tariff and maintenance fund

1. Choose a fee that covers routine service, spare parts, and a reserve.
2. Decide whether households, institutions, and businesses pay differently.
3. Issue receipts, keep a cash book, and post a monthly summary.
4. Audit the account publicly at least twice a year.

### 4. Meet, repair, and resolve disputes

1. Hold a regular community meeting with minutes.
2. Log every repair, cost, and downtime; review the log monthly.
3. Resolve disputes through a defined process before they escalate.
4. Review the rules every two to three years or after a major failure.

## Safety Notes

- **Green hazard:** Governance work is low physical risk but can generate
  conflict. Hold meetings in public, keep records, and avoid capture by one
  family or faction.
- Do not cut off water to households that cannot pay without a written
  hardship process.
- Protect the source from contamination and keep sanitation away from intakes.
- This is a governance guide, not legal advice; confirm water rights locally.
