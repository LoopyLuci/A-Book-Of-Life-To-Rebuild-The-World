# Seismic Bracing

> **Domain:** construction | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** yellow

## Overview

Most earthquake injuries come from falling objects, failed chimneys, and houses
sliding off their foundations, not from the ground opening up. Bracing a house
means tying it to its foundation, strengthening weak walls, and securing heavy
contents. The work must suit the building type; retrofits that look strong but
create a stiff, brittle storey can make damage worse.

## Prerequisites

- Structural assessment of the building
- Carpentry, masonry, and anchoring skills
- A plan approved by a local engineer where required

## Materials & Tools

- Foundation anchor bolts, plates, and epoxy
- Metal straps, brackets, and hold-downs
- Structural screws and nails
- Plywood or oriented strand board for shear walls
- Water heater straps and flexible gas connectors
- Cabinet latches and shelf restraints
- Drill, impact driver, wrench, and level

## Procedure

### 1. Assess and plan

1. Identify the foundation type, wall construction, roof, and any soft storey.
2. Check for existing rot, cracks, and prior unpermitted alterations.
3. Mark the load path from roof to foundation; every brace must connect.
4. Have an engineer review plans for unreinforced masonry or hillside sites.

### 2. Tie the house down

1. Install foundation anchors through the sill plate at the spacing specified
   by the engineer.
2. Add hold-downs at corners and wall ends where uplift and racking are highest.
3. Strap the roof to the walls and the walls to the floor framing.
4. Reinforce cripple walls with plywood sheathing and blocking.

### 3. Brace chimneys and heavy contents

1. Brace or replace unreinforced masonry chimneys; secure the flue.
2. Strap the water heater to wall studs with two metal straps and keep the gas
   connector flexible.
3. Anchor bookcases, cabinets, and tall furniture to studs.
4. Store heavy items low and latch cabinet doors.

### 4. Check and maintain

1. Inspect anchors and straps after any earthquake or nearby construction.
2. Look for new cracks, loose bolts, and water damage.
3. Re-tighten fasteners to the specified torque; do not overtighten wood.
4. Keep a written retrofit plan and photographs for future repairs.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Retrofitting involves power tools, dust, and work at height.
  Wear eye and hearing protection and use stable ladders.
- Do not cut structural members without engineering advice.
- Gas leaks and damaged chimneys are fire and explosion hazards after a quake.
- Unreinforced masonry can collapse suddenly; keep people out of the work zone.
