# Simple Housing

> **Domain:** construction | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** green

## Overview

Permanent shelter protects against weather, pests, and temperature swings. A dry, ventilated, raised-floor structure with a sound roof and a hearth is enough for most climates.

## Prerequisites

- Emergency Shelter
- Rope & Cordage

## Materials & Tools

- Posts and beams (rot-resistant timber or treated poles)
- Wattle, cordwood, adobe, or log infill
- Roof thatch, shingles, or earth
- Stone or rubble for footings

## Procedure

### Site & Footings

1. Choose level, well-drained ground above flood lines.
2. Clear vegetation and mark a rectangle with corner stakes and string.
3. Set corner posts on dry-stacked stone footings to keep wood off the soil.

### Frame & Walls

1. Set vertical posts at 1.5-2 m spacing and brace them plumb.
2. Add a top plate and gable rafters.
3. Infill walls with wattle-and-daub, cordwood, or stacked logs.

### Roof & Floor

1. Lay purlins across the rafters and cover with thatch, shingles, or sod.
2. Overlap roofing layers so water always runs downhill.
3. Raise the floor 20-30 cm on joists and pack the crawl space with dry stone.

## Safety Notes

- Never burn unseasoned or resinous wood in a sealed room without ventilation.
- Treat all framing against insects and rot where possible.
