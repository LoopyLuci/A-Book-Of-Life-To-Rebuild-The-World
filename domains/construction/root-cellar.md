# Root Cellar Construction

> **Domain:** construction | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

A root cellar uses earth's stable temperature and high humidity to keep root
crops, apples, cabbages, and preserves through winter. A 2 m x 3 m cellar holds
roughly 1,500-2,500 kg. The design must balance insulation, ventilation, and
moisture: too warm and crops sprout or rot, too dry and they shrivel, too wet
and mould wins.

## Prerequisites

- Simple housing and earthwork experience
- A well-drained site

## Materials & Tools

- Stone or concrete block
- Timber or concrete roof
- Insulation
- Vent pipes with insect screen
- Gravel floor
- Shelving and slatted crates
- Thermometer and hygrometer
- Rodent mesh

## Procedure

### 1. Site and design

1. Choose a slope or high spot with good drainage; never a hollow that floods.
2. Keep the cellar away from septic fields, wells, and tree roots.
3. Plan two vents: a low inlet and a high outlet for natural convection.
4. Size for the harvest: about 0.5-0.7 tonnes per cubic metre of usable space.

### 2. Excavate and line

1. Mark the footprint and excavate with stable, sloped sides.
2. Shore the walls before digging deeper than 1 m.
3. Build block or stone walls with a footing; leave weep holes and a gravel
   drain.
4. Seal the outside against surface water and insulate the roof and upper
   walls.

### 3. Roof, door, and vents

1. Build a strong roof that can carry soil and snow load.
2. Fit an insulated, tight-fitting door; avoid a direct path for warm air.
3. Install screened vents with adjustable dampers.
4. Cover the roof with earth and vegetation, leaving the vents clear.

### 4. Load and manage

1. Cure root crops for a few days before storage; remove damaged produce.
2. Store in slatted crates off the floor, separated by crop and date.
3. Keep the temperature near 0-4 C and humidity at 85-95%.
4. Vent at night when outside air is colder; close vents in warm weather.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Excavations collapse. Shore walls and never let anyone work
  alone below ground.
- Carbon dioxide can accumulate in a sealed cellar. Open the door and ventilate
  before entering; use a lantern test or gas monitor.
- Mouldy produce releases spores; remove spoilage and wear a mask.
- Keep rodents out with mesh and concrete; they contaminate food and gnaw
  wiring.
