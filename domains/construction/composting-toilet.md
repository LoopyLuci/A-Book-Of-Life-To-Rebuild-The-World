# Composting Toilet

> **Domain:** construction | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

A composting toilet stores human waste with enough carbon cover and time for
pathogens to die off while it decomposes. Twin vaults let one batch cure while
the other is in use. The design must control moisture, ventilation, and
leachate; a smelly or wet toilet is a sign that the process is failing.

## Prerequisites

- Latrine siting and sanitation knowledge
- Basic masonry or carpentry skills
- A source of dry carbon cover

## Materials & Tools

- Concrete, brick, or rot-resistant timber for twin vaults
- Impermeable vault floor and leachate collection
- Vent pipe with fly screen and rain cap
- Toilet pedestal and urine-diverting seat if used
- Sawdust, ash, dry leaves, or straw cover
- Lid, steps, and handwashing station
- Gloves, boots, and cleaning tools
- Thermometer and moisture test

## Procedure

### 1. Site and design

1. Keep the toilet at least 30 m from wells and surface water, and downslope of
   drinking-water sources.
2. Choose a site above the water table and away from flood paths.
3. Size each vault for at least one year of use by the household.
4. Provide access for emptying and a separate handwashing point.

### 2. Build the vaults

1. Cast or lay a level, impermeable floor with a slight slope to a leachate
   sump.
2. Build two sealed vaults with removable covers and a shared pedestal that can
   be switched when one fills.
3. Run a vent pipe from each vault to above the roof; screen the top and keep it
   open.
4. Fit a fly-proof seat and a tight lid; keep rainwater out.

### 3. Operate the toilet

1. Add a cup of dry cover after every use; keep a bucket beside the seat.
2. Divert urine separately if the design allows; do not flood the vault.
3. Keep the vault moist like a wrung-out sponge, not wet.
4. Empty the full vault only after it has cured for 12-24 months.

### 4. Cure and use the product

1. Move cured material to a covered curing bin; protect it from rain and
   animals.
2. Test temperature and moisture; turn the pile if it is too wet.
3. Use finished compost on perennial crops or ornamental plants, not on
   ready-to-eat vegetables.
4. Clean tools and hands, and change clothes after handling.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Human waste carries pathogens. Wear gloves and boots, keep
  the site fly-proof, and wash hands after every contact.
- Leachate can contaminate groundwater; collect and treat it separately.
- Do not use fresh or partially cured material on food crops.
- Vaults can accumulate methane and ammonia; ventilate before opening and never
  enter a vault.
