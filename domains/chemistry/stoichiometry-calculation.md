# Stoichiometry and Limiting Reagents

> **Domain:** chemistry | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Stoichiometry turns a balanced equation into quantities you can weigh out.
Every recipe depends on it: too little of one reagent wastes the other, too
much wastes both. The key principle is that atoms are conserved, so the
coefficients in a balanced equation fix every ratio that follows from it.

## Prerequisites

- None

## Materials & Tools

- A periodic table that lists atomic masses
- A balance that reads to 0.01 g
- Graduated cylinders and at least one volumetric flask
- A calculator with a powers function
- Practice reagents, such as sodium chloride and clean water

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Balance the Equation

1. Write the unbalanced formula for the reaction.
2. Count the atoms of each element on both sides.
3. Add coefficients, never subscripts, until the counts match.
4. Divide every coefficient by their greatest common factor.
5. Re-count the atoms to confirm the balance.

### Stage 2: Convert Masses to Moles

1. Add up the atomic masses for each formula (NaCl = 58.44 g/mol).
2. Divide the mass you have by that molar mass to get moles.
3. Convert volumes to moles with n = M × V, V in litres.
4. Carry at least three significant figures through every step.

### Stage 3: Find the Limiting Reagent

1. Divide the moles of each reactant by its coefficient in the equation.
2. The smallest result is the limiting reagent.
3. The other reactant is in excess.
4. Carry the limiting reagent forward to the product calculation.

### Stage 4: Scale the Batch and Report Yield

1. Multiply the limiting moles by the product coefficient.
2. Convert product moles to grams with the product molar mass.
3. Compare the actual mass to the theoretical mass for percent yield.
4. Scale up or down by multiplying every quantity by the same factor.

### Worked Example

1. React 10.0 g of zinc with 15.0 g of hydrochloric acid.
2. Balance it: Zn + 2 HCl gives ZnCl₂ + H₂.
3. Zinc: 10.0 / 65.38 = 0.1530 mol, divided by 1 gives 0.1530.
4. Acid: 15.0 / 36.46 = 0.4114 mol, divided by 2 gives 0.2057.
5. Zinc is limiting, so hydrogen is 0.1530 mol, or 0.308 g.

## Safety Notes

- Wear eye protection whenever you weigh or transfer reagents.
- Never taste or smell a chemical to identify it.
- Neutralise acid spills with baking soda and base spills with vinegar.
- Keep the balance dry and wipe up reagent spills immediately.
- Do not scale a reaction past what the glassware and cooling can hold.
