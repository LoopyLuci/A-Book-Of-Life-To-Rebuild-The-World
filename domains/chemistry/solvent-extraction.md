# Solvent Extraction

> **Domain:** chemistry | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Liquid-liquid extraction moves a dissolved substance from one liquid into
another that will not mix with the first. It is how you wash a product,
recover an acid from brine, and purify a crude oil. The key principle is
the distribution coefficient, the ratio of concentration in the organic
phase to the concentration in the aqueous phase.

## Prerequisites

- Organic Functional Groups
- Acid-Base Equilibria and Buffering

## Materials & Tools

- A 250 mL separatory funnel with a ground glass stopper
- Ring stand, clamp, and a beaker to catch drips
- Erlenmeyer flasks for each layer
- Immiscible pairs: diethyl ether with water, hexane with water, or
  dichloromethane with water
- Dilute hydrochloric acid and dilute sodium hydroxide for pH control
- Anhydrous sodium sulfate, a pH indicator strip, and a timer
- Goggles, nitrile gloves, and a lab coat or apron

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Choose the Solvent Pair

1. The two liquids must be immiscible; water and an organic solvent are.
2. The solute must be more soluble in the extractant than in the original.
3. Prefer a solvent that is inert, low in toxicity, and easy to strip off.
4. Check the density: the layer with the lower density floats on top.
5. Diethyl ether is 0.71, hexane 0.66, and water 1.00 grams per cubic
   centimetre, so ether and hexane are the top layers.

### Stage 2: Perform the Extraction

1. Put the solution in the funnel and fill it no more than two-thirds.
2. Add the extractant, then stopper and invert the funnel.
3. Open the stopcock to vent, close it, and shake gently for 30 seconds.
4. Vent again, then shake more firmly for a full minute.
5. Set the funnel on a ring and allow 5 to 15 minutes for the layers to
   separate.
6. Drain the lower layer into a flask, then pour the upper layer off
   through the top.

### Stage 3: Wash and Dry

1. Wash the organic layer with a small portion of brine or water.
2. Transfer it into a clean conical flask with a little drying agent.
3. Swirl until the drying agent stops clinging to the glass.
4. Pour off the clear liquid from the drying agent.
5. Repeat the wash if the organic layer still shows a water emulsion.

### Stage 4: Control the pH for Ionisable Compounds

1. To extract an acid, lower the pH below its pKa by at least two units.
2. To extract a base, raise the pH above its pKa by at least two units.
3. Extract the neutral form, then return the aqueous layer to its ionic
   form with dilute acid or base.
4. Use three smaller extractions rather than one large one; with a
   distribution coefficient of 10, three small ones recover about 99
   percent against about 91 percent for a single large extraction.

### Stage 5: Back-Extract and Recover

1. Combine the organic layers and dry them fully.
2. Strip the solvent with a rotary evaporator or a simple distillation.
3. Do not evaporate diethyl ether to dryness; it forms explosive peroxides.
4. Weigh the recovered product and store it in a closed container.

## Safety Notes

- Diethyl ether has a flash point near -45 °C and ignites from a spark;
  use it only with ventilation and away from every flame.
- Dichloromethane and hexane are toxic, and hexane can cause nerve damage
  after long exposure.
- Vent the funnel often; pressure builds up when shaking a closed vessel.
- Wear goggles and nitrile gloves, and change gloves between solvents.
- Keep the work area clear of ignition sources and use only equipment
  rated for flammable solvents.
- Any extraction using carcinogenic or highly toxic solvents needs
  appropriate training, supervision, and equipment.
