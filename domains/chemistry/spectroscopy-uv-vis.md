# UV-Visible Spectrophotometry

> **Domain:** chemistry | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

A spectrophotometer measures how much light a solution absorbs, and that
number is proportional to how much of a substance is dissolved. The method
turns a colour change into a concentration, so it supports water testing,
food analysis, and process control. The key principle is the Beer-Lambert
law, absorbance equals the extinction coefficient times the path length
times the concentration.

## Prerequisites

- None

## Materials & Tools

- A UV-Visible spectrophotometer reading 200 to 800 nm
- Matched cuvettes, quartz for work below 350 nm, plastic above it
- 10, 25, 50, and 100 mL volumetric flasks with a stopper
- Volumetric pipettes or a calibrated dropper and a small cylinder
- A stock standard of known concentration, and the same solvent used to
  prepare the standards
- A cuvette cleaning brush and lint-free tissue

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Prepare the Standards

1. Make a stock solution of known concentration, for example 100 mL of
   1.0 x 10⁻³ M potassium permanganate.
2. Dilute it by pipette into flasks to give 5 or 6 known concentrations.
3. Include a flask of pure solvent as the blank.
4. Label each flask with its concentration and date.

### Stage 2: Blank the Instrument

1. Fill a clean cuvette with the same solvent used for the samples.
2. Wipe the outside walls, place it in the holder, and close the lid.
3. Zero the absorbance at the chosen wavelength.
4. Re-blank every 10 to 15 minutes during a long run.
5. Use the same cuvette for the blank and the sample when you can.

### Stage 3: Measure the Absorbance

1. Pour the sample into a clean cuvette without touching the optical faces.
2. Fill at least two-thirds of the cell and check there are no bubbles.
3. Wipe the outside walls with lint-free tissue before inserting it.
4. Read the absorbance at the wavelength of maximum absorption.
5. Repeat the reading twice and take the mean.
6. Check against theory: permanganate at 525 nm has an extinction
   coefficient near 2200 litres per mole per centimetre, so 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ M
   in a 1 cm cell should read about 0.44.

### Stage 4: Build the Standard Curve

1. Plot absorbance against concentration for the standards.
2. Fit a straight line and note the slope and the intercept.
3. Check that the intercept is close to zero; a large one means a dirty
   blank or contaminated solvent.
4. Keep samples with absorbance between 0.1 and 1.0 for best accuracy.
5. Dilute anything stronger and multiply the result back.

### Stage 5: Measure an Unknown

1. Read the unknown absorbance and check it falls in the usable range.
2. Interpolate the concentration from the standard curve.
3. Multiply by the dilution factor to get the original concentration.
4. Confirm with a second dilution of the same unknown.
5. Report the value with the same number of significant figures as the
   calibration.

## Safety Notes

- Never look into the instrument's light source; some lamps emit UV that
  damages the eye.
- Keep the sample compartment closed during a reading.
- Choose a low-toxicity solvent and wear gloves when handling it.
- Glass cuvettes break; use a plastic cell if the method allows it.
- Do not pipette by mouth and wash your hands after handling samples.
- Dispose of solvent waste in the labelled stream, never down the drain.
