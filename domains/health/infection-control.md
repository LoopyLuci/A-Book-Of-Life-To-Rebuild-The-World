# Infection Control

> **Domain:** health | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

## Overview

Infection control is a set of small, repeated actions: clean hands, clean
surfaces, correct protective equipment, safe sharps, and separation of sick and
well people. In a clinic or a home, these actions prevent more illness than
most medicines. The chain of infection breaks when one link is removed
consistently.

## Prerequisites

- Basic hygiene and first aid
- Understanding of clean versus contaminated

## Materials & Tools

- Soap and clean running water
- Alcohol-based hand rub
- Gloves, masks, eye protection, and gowns
- Disinfectant with a known dilution
- Sharps container
- Sterilizer or pressure cooker with a gauge
- Waste bags and labels
- Handwashing station and towels

## Procedure

### 1. Set up clean zones

1. Separate clean supplies, patient care areas, and waste handling.
2. Keep a handwashing station at every entrance and near every treatment area.
3. Remove jewellery and keep fingernails short; cover cuts with waterproof
   dressings.
4. Stock PPE where it is used, not in a locked cupboard across the building.

### 2. Practice hand hygiene

1. Wash with soap and water when hands are visibly dirty or after body-fluid
   contact.
2. Use alcohol rub before and after every patient contact when hands are not
   visibly soiled.
3. Clean hands before gloves, after glove removal, and after touching shared
   surfaces.
4. Dry hands with single-use towels and keep the station stocked.

### 3. Use barriers and safe sharps

1. Wear gloves for blood, body fluids, and non-intact skin; change between
   patients.
2. Add a mask, eye protection, and gown when splashes are likely.
3. Never recap, bend, or break needles; place them directly in a sharps
   container.
4. Remove PPE without touching the outside, then wash hands.

### 4. Clean, disinfect, and sterilize

1. Clean visible soil with detergent before disinfection or sterilization.
2. Use the correct contact time and dilution for the disinfectant.
3. Sterilize reusable instruments with a validated pressure-cooker or autoclave
   cycle; record temperature, pressure, and time.
4. Separate clean and dirty instruments and transport them in closed containers.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Blood and body fluids can transmit HIV, hepatitis, and
  other infections. Treat every patient as potentially infectious.
- Alcohol rub does not kill bacterial spores; use sterilization for instruments.
- Never reuse gloves or single-use needles.
- Disinfectants can irritate skin and lungs; ventilate and follow the label.
