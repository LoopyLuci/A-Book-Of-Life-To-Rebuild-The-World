# Mass Casualty Triage

> **Domain:** health | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Triage sorts injured people by urgency when demand exceeds resources. It is not
a diagnosis: it is a repeated, rapid decision about who needs treatment first
and who can wait. The goal is to do the greatest good for the greatest number,
not to treat the most dramatic injury first.

## Prerequisites

- Basic first aid
- A practised triage method and clear roles

## Materials & Tools

- Colour-coded triage tags
- Permanent markers
- Gloves and eye protection
- Headlamp
- Bandages and tourniquets
- Clipboard and tally sheet
- Radio or runner system

## Procedure

### 1. Make the scene safe

1. Stop and assess hazards: traffic, fire, structural collapse, violence.
2. Do not become a casualty; wait for the scene to be secured if needed.
3. Identify a triage officer and a treatment area away from the hazard.
4. Establish one-way flow: triage, treatment, transport.

### 2. Triage each casualty

1. Use a rapid method such as START: check ability to walk, breathing,
   perfusion, and mental status.
2. Tag immediate (red) for life threats needing treatment within minutes,
   delayed (yellow) for serious but stable, minor (green) for walking wounded,
   and deceased (black) after confirmation.
3. Control catastrophic bleeding with direct pressure or a tourniquet during
   the primary sweep.
4. Spend no more than 30-60 seconds per casualty in the initial triage.

### 3. Reassess and treat

1. Re-triage after movement or deterioration; tags are not permanent.
2. Treat red casualties in order of likely benefit, not arrival order.
3. Keep airways open, stop bleeding, and prevent hypothermia.
4. Record identity, tag colour, time, and interventions.

### 4. Transport and communicate

1. Match transport priority to triage colour and destination capability.
2. Send a tally of casualties to the receiving facility.
3. Keep families informed through a single, trusted source.
4. Debrief the response and rest the team in shifts.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Triage errors cost lives. Practise and use a recognised
  method.
- Use gloves and eye protection; assume all blood and body fluids are
  infectious.
- Do not enter an unstable structure or an active threat scene.
- Psychological stress is cumulative. Rotate roles and provide support.
