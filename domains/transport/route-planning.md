# Route Planning

> **Domain:** transport | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

A route plan turns a destination into a schedule with known water, feed, fuel,
load limits, and escape options. The shortest line on a map is often a steep,
seasonal track that destroys a cart or exhausts an animal. Good planning uses
contour lines, local knowledge, and a margin for delays.

## Prerequisites

- Map reading and compass skills
- Knowledge of the vehicle, cart, or animal
- Communication plan

## Materials & Tools

- Topographic maps and compass or GPS
- Route card, pencil, and weatherproof cover
- Vehicle or cart maintenance checklist
- Water and feed containers
- Tire repair kit or spare parts
- Radio or charged phone
- Local contacts and permits

## Procedure

### 1. Gather route information

1. Mark the origin, destination, and any required stops on a topographic map.
2. Read contours, stream crossings, seasonal roads, and grades; avoid slopes
   beyond the safe limit for the load.
3. Ask local users about washouts, landslides, snow, and restricted areas.
4. Check permits, tolls, and border or checkpoint requirements.

### 2. Calculate time and supplies

1. Estimate distance and average speed for the actual surface and load.
2. Add time for grades, river crossings, rest, and animal welfare.
3. Plan water, food, and fuel for at least one extra day.
4. Identify a turn-around point and a second route.

### 3. Prepare the load and team

1. Confirm axle, bridge, and vehicle weight limits; weigh the load if possible.
2. Brief every traveler on the route, checkpoints, and emergency signals.
3. Leave a copy of the route card with someone who is not traveling.
4. Check tires, brakes, harnesses, and spare parts before departure.

### 4. Travel and adapt

1. Log actual times, water sources, and road conditions at each stop.
2. Turn back at the planned point if weather or road conditions deteriorate.
3. Avoid flooded roads, fresh landslide debris, and unmarked ice.
4. Update the route card for the next traveler.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Remote routes combine exposure, vehicle failure, and
  uncertain water. Carry communication and shelter even on a day trip.
- Do not cross a flooded road or bridge; depth and current are hard to judge.
- Steep grades can overheat brakes or exhaust animals; use low gear and rest.
- Checkpoints and closed areas may involve security risks; follow local advice.
