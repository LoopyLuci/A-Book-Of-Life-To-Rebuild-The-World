# Handcarts and Two-Wheeled Carts

> **Domain:** transport | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

## Overview

A two-wheeled cart multiplies human carrying capacity several times over. On
firm, level ground one person can move 100-300 kg; on rough tracks, two people
can move 50-100 kg safely. The key details are a strong axle, wheels that roll
freely, a low centre of gravity, and a brake for slopes.

## Prerequisites

- Basic woodworking or metalworking
- Wheels with bearings, or the ability to make and maintain them

## Materials & Tools

- Hardwood or steel axle
- Two wheels with bearings and inflated tyres
- Timber bed and side rails
- Bolts, washers, and locking nuts
- Brake or drag shoe
- Handle or harness
- Grease and a weatherproof cover

## Procedure

### 1. Design for the load

1. Set the bed length so the load sits over the axle, not behind it.
2. Keep the bed low; a high load tips easily on side slopes.
3. Use a wide track for rough ground and a narrow track for paths and doors.
4. Build for at least twice the intended load to survive potholes and braking.

### 2. Build the chassis

1. Mount the axle under the bed with bearing blocks or steel brackets.
2. Fit wheels so they spin freely without rubbing the frame.
3. Brace the bed diagonally to resist twisting.
4. Fit a removable handle or a padded harness for pulling.

### 3. Add control and braking

1. Fit a drag shoe, band brake, or a rope brake for downhill loads.
2. Use a hitch pin that cannot bounce out on rough ground.
3. Round and pad every surface that contacts hands or shoulders.
4. Add side rails or lashing points for loose cargo.

### 4. Load and test

1. Test empty on level ground, then with half load.
2. Load heavy items over the axle and low in the bed.
3. Secure the load with two independent ties.
4. Check tyre pressure, axle nuts, and brakes before every trip.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** A loaded cart can run away on a slope. Always use a brake
  and keep the load balanced.
- Do not carry passengers unless the cart has seats and guards.
- Use reflective markers or a lamp on roads; carts are low and hard to see.
- Avoid overload on soft ground; it causes injury and destroys tracks.
