# River Ferries and Small-Boat Crossings

> **Domain:** transport | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

## Overview

A river crossing can connect a community to markets, clinics, and farmland, but
moving water is unforgiving. A small ferry or cable-guided raft is safest when
it is built for a known load, operated by trained people, and shut down during
floods. Freeboard, balance, and a reliable cable matter more than speed.

## Prerequisites

- Boat-building or raft-building skills
- A river survey at low and high water
- Anchors, cable, and pulley hardware

## Materials & Tools

- Boat or raft hull with sealed buoyancy
- Steel cable or strong rope
- Pulleys, anchors, and shackles
- Life jackets or approved floats
- Bailer and throw line
- Oars, pole, or outboard engine
- Loading ramp and non-slip deck

## Procedure

### 1. Survey the crossing

1. Measure river width, depth, and current at low and high water.
2. Identify upstream hazards: dams, snags, rapids, and sudden releases.
3. Choose a crossing line with a safe landing on both banks.
4. Set a clear flood rule: no crossing above a marked water level.

### 2. Build or select the craft

1. Size the hull for the intended load plus a safety margin.
2. Add sealed buoyancy tanks or foam so a swamped boat still floats.
3. Fit strong attachment points for the cable or pulleys.
4. Provide handrails, non-slip deck, and a loading ramp.

### 3. Rig the cable or propulsion

1. Anchor the cable upstream of the crossing so current helps hold the craft.
2. Use pulleys rated well above the expected load and inspect shackles daily.
3. Keep a sharp knife at the cable attachment for emergency release.
4. Mark the cable and keep it below head height at landings.

### 4. Operate safely

1. Count passengers and load before departure; never exceed the marked limit.
2. Distribute weight evenly and keep passengers seated.
3. Require floats for children and non-swimmers.
4. Cancel crossings in floods, darkness, high wind, or poor visibility.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Overloading or a shifted load can capsize the craft. Drowning
  is fast and quiet.
- Cable and pulley failure can snap back with lethal force. Stand clear and
  inspect hardware before each day.
- Cold water causes rapid incapacitation. Wear a float and keep dry clothing
  in a sealed bag.
- Never tie a passenger to the craft or to the cable.
