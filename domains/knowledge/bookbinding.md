# Bookbinding and Repair

> **Domain:** knowledge | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

## Overview

Books survive only if their binding is repaired before pages are lost. Simple
pamphlet stitching, case binding, and spine repair need few tools and can be
taught quickly. A binder can produce 1-20 books a day depending on style,
drying time, and how much work is repair rather than new binding.

## Prerequisites

- Paper making or access to suitable paper
- Basic hand-tool skills

## Materials & Tools

- Paper signatures
- Linen thread and wax
- Book cloth or leather
- Binder's board
- PVA or cooked starch paste
- Endpapers
- Bone folder, awl, needle, and knife
- Book press or weighted boards

## Procedure

### 1. Prepare the text block

1. Fold sheets into signatures with the grain running vertically.
2. Collate pages in order and check for missing or reversed sheets.
3. Press the folded signatures overnight to flatten the folds.
4. Mark sewing stations along the spine with an awl.

### 2. Sew the book

1. Sew through the stations with waxed linen thread; keep tension even.
2. Link each signature to the previous one so the block holds together.
3. Tie off securely and press the sewn block.
4. Glue the spine with a flexible adhesive and line it with cloth or paper.

### 3. Make the case

1. Cut two boards and a spine strip to size, allowing a small square at the
   edges.
2. Cover the boards with cloth or leather, working from the centre outward.
3. Turn in the edges neatly and press the case flat.
4. Attach the text block with endpapers, keeping the spine flexible.

### 4. Repair and protect

1. Tighten loose hinges with a thin line of PVA and press.
2. Repair torn pages with Japanese tissue and starch paste.
3. Reback a broken spine with new cloth, not household tape.
4. Store upright, dry, and out of direct sun; keep pests away.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Knives and awls cause deep puncture wounds. Cut away from
  the body and keep tools sharp.
- Use glue in a ventilated area; solvent-based adhesives are toxic.
- Repetitive folding and sewing strain hands and wrists; take breaks.
- Damp books grow mould. Dry wet books immediately and separate pages.
