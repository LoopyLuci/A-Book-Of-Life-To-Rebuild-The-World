# Cold Exposure and Hypothermia

> **Domain:** survival | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** red

## Overview

Cold kills by lowering the body's core temperature. Wet clothing, wind, and
exhaustion accelerate heat loss far faster than cold air alone. Hypothermia
begins with shivering and clumsy hands, progresses to confusion and slurred
speech, and can end in cardiac arrest. The priority is to stop heat loss and
warm the core, not to warm the extremities first.

## Prerequisites

- Emergency shelter
- Fire or another heat source

## Materials & Tools

- Dry insulating layers and a windproof outer layer
- Warm blankets or a sleeping bag
- Fire-starting kit
- Warm, sweet fluids if the person is fully alert
- Emergency shelter or windbreak

## Procedure

### 1. Recognize the condition

1. Look for uncontrolled shivering, numbness, clumsy movement, confusion, or
   drowsiness.
2. Check responsiveness and breathing; treat cardiac arrest with CPR and call
   for help.
3. Handle the person gently; rough movement can trigger an irregular heartbeat
   in severe hypothermia.
4. Assume hypothermia in any wet, exhausted person in cold or windy conditions.

### 2. Stop heat loss

1. Move to shelter, out of wind and rain.
2. Remove wet clothing by cutting it off if necessary; keep the person
   horizontal.
3. Insulate from the ground with mats, packs, or dry branches.
4. Cover the head, neck, and torso; use a vapour barrier if available.

### 3. Warm the core

1. Apply skin-to-skin heat or warm blankets to the chest, neck, and groin.
2. Give warm, sweet drinks only if the person is fully conscious and can
   swallow; never give alcohol.
3. Use a fire or heater to warm the shelter, not to directly roast the skin.
4. Evacuate severe cases; do not attempt vigorous rewarming in the field.

### 4. Manage frostbite

1. Do not rub snow, massage, or walk on frozen feet.
2. Rewarm only when there is no risk of refreezing, using warm water around
   37-39 C.
3. Protect blisters and separate fingers and toes with dry gauze.
4. Seek medical care; frostbite can look harmless before tissue dies.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Severe hypothermia can cause cardiac arrest. Handle the
  person gently and evacuate.
- Alcohol increases heat loss and impairs judgement; never use it to warm
  someone.
- Do not apply direct heat to numb skin; burns are common.
- Rewarming a frostbitten limb that may freeze again causes more damage than
  waiting.
