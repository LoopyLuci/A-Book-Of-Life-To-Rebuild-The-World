# Rope & Cordage

> **Domain:** survival | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Cordage is a force multiplier — needed for shelter, traps, fire-making, and
construction. Any fibrous plant can become cordage.

## Prerequisites

- None

## Materials & Tools

- Fibrous plants: nettle, hemp, flax, yucca, inner bark of basswood/willow
- Hands

## Procedure

### Extracting Fiber

1. Harvest plant stems at the end of the growing season.
2. Ret in water 1–3 weeks until bark slips easily.
3. Peel fiber strips lengthwise.
4. Dry in shade to prevent brittleness.

### Reverse-Wrap (Two-Strand Twist)

1. Split fiber into two roughly equal strands.
2. Twist strand A clockwise.
3. Wrap strand B counter-clockwise over strand A.
4. Repeat, alternating, keeping tension.
5. Splice in new fiber by overlapping ends and twisting together.

### Three-Strand Braid

1. Anchor three strands.
2. Cross right over middle, then left over middle.
3. Repeat. Stronger and more uniform than two-strand.

### Making Rope from Vines

1. Score a vine with a rock and peel in long strips.
2. Soak in water for flexibility.
3. Braid 3–4 strips together.

## Safety Notes

- Test cordage load before trusting it with weight.
- Nettle requires gloves when harvesting.
- Retting water smells; do it away from camp.
