# Water Purification

> **Domain:** survival | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** red

## Overview

Unsafe water causes cholera, typhoid, dysentery, and giardiasis. Purification
requires addressing three classes of contaminants: biological, chemical, and
particulate.

## Prerequisites

- Making Fire (for boiling method)

## Materials & Tools

- Container (metal or ceramic)
- Filter medium (sand, charcoal, cloth)
- Chlorine bleach (unscented, 5–6% sodium hypochlorite)
- UV source (sunlight in PET bottles)

## Procedure

### Method 1: Boiling

1. Bring water to a rolling boil.
2. Maintain boil for at least 1 minute (3 minutes above 2000 m altitude).
3. Cool covered to prevent recontamination.

### Method 2: Sand-Charcoal Filter

1. Cut the bottom off a plastic bottle.
2. Layer (bottom to top): cloth, charcoal, sand, gravel.
3. Pour water through slowly.
4. **Follow with boiling or chlorination** — filters do not kill viruses.

### Method 3: Chlorination

1. Use unscented household bleach (5–6% sodium hypochlorite).
2. Add 2 drops per liter of clear water; 4 drops if cloudy.
3. Stir and wait 30 minutes.
4. Water should have a slight chlorine smell. If not, repeat.

### Method 4: SODIS (Solar Disinfection)

1. Fill clear PET bottles with water.
2. Lay on reflective surface in full sun.
3. Expose for 6 hours (or 2 days if cloudy).
4. Drink directly from the bottle.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Incorrect chlorination can cause poisoning.
- Never use scented bleach or bleach with additives.
- Chemical contamination (pesticides, heavy metals) requires distillation or
  activated carbon — do not rely on boiling.
