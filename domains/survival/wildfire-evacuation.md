# Wildfire Evacuation

> **Domain:** survival | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** red

## Overview

Wildfire evacuation succeeds when a household leaves before roads close and
smoke makes driving dangerous. The plan must name trigger points, two routes,
a meeting place, and what to carry. Defensible space buys time for the house
but never replaces early departure; most deaths occur while people are still
preparing to leave or are caught on a road.

## Prerequisites

- A household meeting place and communication plan
- Basic first aid and fire awareness

## Materials & Tools

- Go bags for each person, including medicines and spare glasses
- N95 or better respirators and eye protection
- Flashlight, headlamp, batteries, and a power bank
- Paper maps with two marked routes
- Battery radio or hand-crank radio
- Water, snacks, and pet supplies
- Copies of identity, insurance, and medical documents
- Sturdy shoes, long sleeves, and cotton or wool layers

## Procedure

### 1. Plan before fire season

1. Draw two evacuation routes from the home and test both in daylight.
2. Choose a meeting place outside the fire zone and a second contact who lives
   farther away.
3. Pack go bags and keep them by the exit; include a week of medicines.
4. Clear defensible space: remove dead vegetation, move firewood, and keep the
   driveway clear for emergency vehicles.
5. Register for local alerts and learn the difference between ready, set, and
   go notices.

### 2. Act on trigger points

1. Leave when the "set" stage is announced, or earlier if smoke is thick, ash
   is falling, or the sky turns orange.
2. Do not wait for a mandatory order if the only road is a narrow canyon or
   ridge route.
3. Close windows and vents, turn off gas if instructed, and leave lights on so
   responders can see the house.
4. Take pets in carriers and livestock only if it can be done without delay.

### 3. Drive out safely

1. Use the route away from the fire, not the shortest route on a map.
2. Keep headlights on and stay below the speed limit; smoke can hide vehicles,
   animals, and downed lines.
3. If trapped in a vehicle, park in a cleared area, close vents, lie below the
   windows, and cover exposed skin.
4. Never drive through flames or over hoses; turn back if the road is blocked.

### 4. Check in and recover

1. Tell the out-of-area contact where you are and who is with you.
2. Do not return until authorities declare the area safe.
3. Photograph damage before cleanup and keep receipts.
4. Replace smoke-damaged food, medicine, and filters; assume well water is
   contaminated until tested.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Wildfire can change direction and speed within minutes.
  Leave early rather than defend a structure.
- Smoke is a serious health hazard, especially for children, older adults, and
  people with heart or lung disease.
- Downed power lines can energize fences, water, and vehicles. Stay clear.
- Embers can ignite roofs and gutters hours after the fire front passes.
