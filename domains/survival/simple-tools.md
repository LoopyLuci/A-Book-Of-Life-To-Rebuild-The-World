## Overview

Tools extend the hand. Before metal, humans made cutting, scraping, and
pounding tools from stone, bone, and wood.

## Prerequisites

- [Rope & Cordage](rope-cordage.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Stone (flint, chert, obsidian for cutting; granite for pounding)
- Wood (hardwood for handles)
- Bone, antler
- Cordage
- Hammerstone

## Procedure

### Flake Knife (Flint Knapping)

1. Hold the core with a leather pad.
2. Strike the edge with a hammerstone at a shallow angle.
3. Detach thin, sharp flakes.
4. Pressure-flake the edge with an antler tine for sharpness.

### Hand Axe

1. Select a fist-sized cobble.
2. Strike alternating flakes from both faces to form a teardrop shape.
3. Sharpen one end; keep the other rounded for grip.

### Stone Hammer

1. Find a round, hard cobble.
2. Split a green hardwood branch, insert the stone, bind with cordage.
3. Tighten as the wood shrinks.

### Digging Stick

1. Cut a 1 m hardwood branch.
2. Fire-harden the tip (hold in embers, then cool slowly).
3. Sharpen with a stone.

### Bone Awl

1. Split a large long bone lengthwise.
2. Grind a point on sandstone.
3. Polish on leather.

## Safety Notes

- Flint knapping produces razor-sharp flakes; wear eye protection and gloves.
- Fire-hardening weakens the wood if overheated; keep it yellow, not charred.
- Stone dust inhalation is hazardous; work outdoors.
