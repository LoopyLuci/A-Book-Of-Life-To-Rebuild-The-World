# Solar Photovoltaic Systems

> **Domain:** energy | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

## Overview

A solar photovoltaic system turns sunlight into electricity with no fuel and
few moving parts. A 1 kW array in a sunny climate produces roughly 3-5 kWh per
day after losses. The panels are only half the system: a charge controller,
battery bank, fuses, and correctly sized cable determine whether it is safe and
long-lived.

## Prerequisites

- Battery basics
- Electrical wiring skills

## Materials & Tools

- PV panels and mounting rails
- Charge controller sized for array current
- Battery bank, vented and secured
- Inverter if alternating current is needed
- Fuses, disconnects, and grounding hardware
- UV-rated cable and MC4 connectors

## Procedure

### 1. Audit the load

1. List every appliance and its watts times hours per day.
2. Add 20-30% for inverter and wiring losses.
3. Size the array for the worst month, not the best.
4. Size the battery for 1-3 days of autonomy, depending on critical loads.

### 2. Mount the array

1. Face the panels toward the equator: south in the northern hemisphere.
2. Tilt them at roughly the site latitude, steeper in winter if snow is common.
3. Keep panels clear of shade from trees, buildings, and each other.
4. Leave airflow behind the panels and secure rails for wind load.

### 3. Wire and protect

1. Connect panels in series or parallel to match the controller's voltage and
   current limits.
2. Fuse every source and use a disconnect at the battery.
3. Use cable rated for the current and UV exposure; crimp and seal connectors.
4. Ground the array frame and equipment according to local code.

### 4. Commission and monitor

1. Check polarity and open-circuit voltage before connecting the controller.
2. Connect the battery first if the controller requires it, then the array.
3. Verify charging current and battery voltage in sun and shade.
4. Record daily output and inspect terminals for corrosion every few months.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** PV panels produce dangerous DC voltage whenever light falls
  on them. Cover them and isolate before wiring.
- Batteries can release hydrogen and acid. Ventilate, wear eye protection, and
  avoid sparks.
- Undersized cable and missing fuses cause fires. Size for continuous current
  and fault current.
- Work on roofs with fall protection; do not work alone.
