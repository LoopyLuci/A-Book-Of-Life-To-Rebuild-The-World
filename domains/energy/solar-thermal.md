## Overview

Solar thermal collects sunlight as heat — for cooking, water heating,
sterilization, and drying. Simpler and cheaper than photovoltaics.

## Prerequisites

- [Simple Tools](../survival/simple-tools.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Reflective surface (mirror, foil, polished metal)
- Absorber (black metal)
- Insulation
- Glass or plastic cover
- Water pipes (for heaters)

## Procedure

### Solar Cooker (Box Type)

1. Build a wooden box 50 × 50 × 30 cm.
2. Line with insulation (straw, newspaper).
3. Blacken the interior base.
4. Glass lid on top.
5. Reflector flap angled to direct sunlight.
6. Temp: 120–150°C. Cooks rice in 2 hours.

### Parabolic Cooker

1. Satellite dish lined with Mylar or mirror strips.
2. Focus point holds a black pot.
3. Temp: 250–400°C. Cooks as fast as a stove.
4. Must be adjusted every 15 minutes.

### Solar Water Heater (Batch)

1. Black tank (50–100 L) in an insulated box with glass top.
2. Cold water enters bottom; hot exits top.
3. Temp: 60–80°C on sunny days.

### Solar Water Heater (Thermosiphon)

1. Flat plate collector: black absorber + copper pipes + glass cover.
2. Tank above collector; thermosiphon circulates water.
3. More efficient than batch.

### Solar Still

1. Pit with black liner.
2. Salty or dirty water in the pit.
3. Glass or plastic cover slopes to a collection channel.
4. Yield: 1–4 L/m²/day.

### Solar Dryer

1. Box with black interior and glass top.
2. Chimney for airflow.
3. Dries fruit, vegetables, meat in 1–3 days.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Parabolic cookers reach 400°C — fires and burns.
  Use gloves, never leave unattended.
- Never look directly at concentrated sunlight.
- Solar cookers work poorly in clouds; have a backup.
