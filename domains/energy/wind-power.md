# Wind Power

> **Domain:** energy | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** yellow

## Overview

Wind turbines convert kinetic energy into electricity or mechanical power.
Small turbines (1–10 kW) can charge batteries or pump water.

- [Simple Tools](../survival/simple-tools.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Blades (wood, fiberglass, or PVC pipe)
- Generator (permanent magnet alternator)
- Tower or mast
- Guy wires
- Charge controller
- Batteries

## Procedure

### Sizing

1. Measure average wind speed at hub height.
2. Power ∝ (wind speed)³ × (rotor area).
3. 1 m diameter rotor ≈ 30 W at 5 m/s; 3 m ≈ 300 W; 5 m ≈ 1 kW.

### Blade Design

1. 3 blades preferred (balance of torque and speed).
2. Tapered and twisted for efficiency.
3. Tip speed ratio (TSR): 5–7 for electricity, 2–3 for water pumping.
4. Pitch: 10–20° at the root, 0° at the tip.

### Alternator (Axial Flux PMG)

1. Two steel disks with magnets (N-S alternating).
2. Stator with coils between them.
3. 3-phase output rectified to DC.
4. Example: 12 magnets, 9 coils → 12 V at 300 RPM.

### Tower

1. Height: 10 m minimum for small turbines.
2. Guy wires at 3 levels.
3. Furling mechanism (tail vane) for overspeed protection.

### Electrical

1. Rectify 3-phase AC → DC.
2. Charge controller prevents overcharge.
3. Dump load (resistor) for excess.
4. Inverter for AC loads.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Blades spin at 300+ RPM — decapitation risk.
- Never touch a spinning rotor.
- Guy wires under tension snap — stay clear.
- Turbine must be furled or braked in storms.
- Lightning protection required.
