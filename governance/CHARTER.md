# Governance Charter — A Book Of Life To Rebuild The World

## Article I — Purpose

A Book Of Life To Rebuild The World exists to preserve and distribute the
knowledge needed to rebuild civilization from first principles. This charter
defines how decisions are made, how contributors are empowered, and how the
project remains free and open.

## Article II — Principles

1. **Openness.** All content, code, and governance are public.
2. **Verifiability.** Claims are sourced; procedures are field-tested.
3. **Decentralization.** Anyone may fork the project without permission.
4. **Subsidiarity.** Decisions are made at the lowest competent level.
5. **Non-violence.** Discourse is respectful; harassment is prohibited.
6. **Stewardship.** The project is held in trust for future generations.

## Article III — Membership

Any person may become a **Contributor** by signing the Contributor License
Agreement (CLA) and submitting a pull request.

Contributors who demonstrate sustained quality may be nominated to one of
the following roles by a Domain Maintainer:

- **Verifier** — field-tests entries and certifies them.
- **Domain Maintainer** — reviews pull requests in one domain.
- **Core Maintainer** — release management, architecture, governance.

## Article IV — Roles & Responsibilities

### Readers
- Browse, search, download, mirror.

### Contributors
- Submit entries, edits, translations, tests, bug fixes.
- Follow the Code of Conduct.
- Sign the CLA.

### Verifiers
- Execute entries in the field.
- Submit field-test reports using the schema.
- Recuse themselves from reviewing their own entries.

### Domain Maintainers
- Review and merge pull requests for one domain.
- Nominate new contributors for verifier status.
- Maintain the domain README index.
- Serve a 12-month term, renewable by consensus of peers.

### Core Maintainers
- Merge cross-domain changes.
- Cut releases (semver).
- Manage CI, security, and infrastructure.
- Ratify charter amendments.
- Minimum of 3, maximum of 9.

## Article V — Decision Making

### Default: Rough Consensus

Decisions are made by rough consensus of the relevant maintainers. Silence
is consent after a 7-day comment period.

### Escalation: Formal Vote

If consensus fails, a formal vote is held:

- Simple majority for operational decisions.
- Two-thirds supermajority for charter amendments.
- Unanimity for changing the license.

Each Core Maintainer has one vote. Domain Maintainers vote on issues in
their domain. Contributors may call for a vote via petition signed by 10
contributors.

### Conflict Resolution

1. Good-faith discussion between parties.
2. Mediation by a Domain Maintainer.
3. Mediation by two Core Maintainers.
4. Binding arbitration by a three-member panel selected by lot.

## Article VI — Verification

1. An entry begins in **draft**.
2. Two reviewers advance it to **peer-reviewed**.
3. A field test advances it to **field-tested**.
4. If superseded, it becomes **deprecated**.
5. All transitions are recorded in the verification ledger.

## Article VII — Licensing

- Content: CC BY-SA 4.0.
- Code: MIT.
- Domain names, logos: reserved, but may be used by mirrors with attribution.

Forks may relicense derivative content under any compatible license.

## Article VIII — Finances

The project accepts donations through an Open Collective or fiscal sponsor.
Funds are used exclusively for:

- Infrastructure (CI, hosting, domain).
- Community tooling.
- Travel stipends for verification field tests.

Financial reports are published quarterly.

## Article IX — Amendments

This charter may be amended by a two-thirds vote of Core Maintainers, after
a 14-day public comment period.

## Article X — Dissolution

If the project is dissolved, all content is archived with the Internet
Archive and Zenodo under its open licenses. Any remaining assets are donated
to a like-minded nonprofit.
