# Analytical Weighing, Standards and Calibration

> **Domain:** chemistry | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Every measurement is only as good as the calibration chain behind it, and
analytical weighing is the root of that chain. Calibrating a balance against
certified standards, recording the state of the instrument, and building
standard curves are what make later results worth trusting. The key principle
is traceability: an instrument's reading must be linked by an unbroken chain
of documented calibrations to a standard whose value is known.

## Prerequisites

- Basic algebra and the ability to read a scatter of data
- A level bench away from vibration, draught, and direct sun
- Access to check masses of a known class

## Materials & Tools

- Analytical balance with 0.1 mg readability on a vibration-free bench
- Working weights in a case, plus OIML class E2 check masses
- Weighing boats or watch glasses, tweezers, and a soft brush
- Draft shield or balance cover, and a spirit level
- Thermometer and hygrometer to record the room conditions
- Lint-free wipes, distilled water, and a dry brush for cleaning
- Logbook with numbered, signed, and dated entries

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Prepare the balance and the room

1. Level the bench and the balance, then let the balance settle and warm up
   for the maker's warm-up time, commonly 30 minutes.
2. Clean the weighing chamber and the pan with a lint-free brush, remove any
   spilled material, and record the room temperature, the humidity, and the
   date of the last service.

### Stage 2: Run the internal check

1. Place the centre-of-gravity check mass on the pan and confirm the reading
   matches the certificate.
2. Run the built-in repeatability test at least three times, record the
   spread, and adjust the level bubble and repeat the check if the internal
   check fails.

### Stage 3: Calibrate against external standards

1. Weigh each check mass in turn, at the mass needed, and record the raw
   reading and the correction you intend to apply.
2. Calculate the correction as the certified value minus the observed reading,
   and fit a straight line of correction against mass when you use more than
   two points.
3. Reject the run if any point deviates by more than the balance's stated
   tolerance, and repeat the calibration at least daily, and after any move,
   any shock, or any change of environment.

### Stage 4: Weigh a sample properly

1. Place the sample in a clean, dry weighing boat and let it reach room
   temperature inside the draft shield before closing it.
2. Record the full reading with its uncertainty once the display settles,
   and log it immediately.

### Stage 5: Build a calibration curve

1. Prepare standards at five or more known concentrations that bracket the
   range you expect to measure.
2. Measure each standard at least twice, then fit a straight line or a
   second-order polynomial to concentration against signal.
3. Interpolate the unknown from the curve, and report the result with its
   uncertainty and the range over which the method is valid.

## Safety Notes

- Analytical balances are delicate. Never slam the draft shield, never weigh
  a hot or wet vessel, and never put chemicals straight onto the pan.
- Weigh very small or static-prone powders in a closed container, because a
  draught will lift the sample out of the boat.
- Keep volatile samples out of the weighing chamber until the reading
  settles, or the mass will drift steadily.
- Never adjust a balance while it is loaded or while a measurement is
  running.
- Clean up spilled mercury or toxic powders at once and follow the site's
  spill procedure.
- Balance oils and dusty powders can be inhaled during cleaning. Use a
  respirator and work in a ventilated space.
