# Basic First Aid

> **Domain:** survival | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Field first aid prioritizes: airway, breathing, circulation (ABC), then
bleeding control, then shock prevention. Definitive care is in
[Health](../../../../domains/health/README.md).

## Prerequisites

- None

## Materials & Tools

- Clean cloth
- Boiled water
- Improvised splints
- Honey (antimicrobial)

## Procedure

### ABC Check

1. **Airway:** tilt head back, lift chin. Clear obstructions.
2. **Breathing:** look, listen, feel for 10 seconds.
3. **Circulation:** check carotid pulse.

### CPR (Adult)

1. 30 chest compressions (5–6 cm deep) at 100–120/min.
2. 2 rescue breaths.
3. Repeat. Continue until help arrives or the person revives.

### Bleeding Control

1. Apply direct pressure with a clean cloth.
2. Add layers without removing the first.
3. If severe, apply a tourniquet 5–7 cm above the wound.
4. Note tourniquet time; do not remove in the field.

### Fracture Splinting

1. Immobilize joints above and below the fracture.
2. Pad the splint.
3. Check distal pulse before and after.

### Burn Care

1. Cool with clean running water for 20 minutes.
2. Cover with a clean, non-stick dressing.
3. Do not pop blisters.
4. Do not apply butter, oil, or ice.

### Shock Prevention

1. Lay the person flat; raise legs 30 cm.
2. Keep warm.
3. Give nothing by mouth if unconscious.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Do not move someone with a suspected spinal injury unless in
  immediate danger.
- Improper CPR can cause rib fractures; correct hand placement matters.
- Tourniquets can cause tissue death; use only for life-threatening bleeding.
