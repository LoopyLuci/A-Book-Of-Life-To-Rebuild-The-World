# Pack Animals and Load Transport

> **Domain:** transport | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Pack animals move goods where wheels cannot go: steep trails, narrow paths, and
stream crossings. A fit animal carries roughly 20-30% of its body weight, or
60-150 kg depending on species, for 10-30 km a day. Good saddles, balanced
loads, and regular rest prevent the sores and exhaustion that end a transport
season early.

## Prerequisites

- Experience handling the chosen species
- Feed, water, and veterinary access along the route

## Materials & Tools

- Pack saddle fitted to the animal
- Saddle pads and blankets
- Panniers or cargo nets
- Ropes and straps
- Hobbles and picket line
- Animal first-aid kit
- Feed and water containers

## Procedure

### 1. Select and condition animals

1. Choose calm, sound animals; check hooves, teeth, and back for sores.
2. Condition gradually with increasing loads over several weeks.
3. Learn the animal's normal behaviour and appetite.
4. Plan water and grazing every day; do not rely on found feed.

### 2. Fit the saddle

1. Place a clean pad over the back, free of wrinkles and dirt.
2. Set the saddle so the load sits behind the shoulders and ahead of the hips.
3. Tighten the girth gradually and check for rubbing.
4. Inspect the back daily; treat hot spots before they become open sores.

### 3. Load and balance

1. Keep the load under 20-30% of body weight, less on steep or soft ground.
2. Split weight evenly left and right; keep heavy items low and close to the
   spine.
3. Secure with a breast strap and breeching so the load does not slide.
4. Cover sharp edges and keep cargo quiet; flapping loads spook animals.

### 4. Travel and rest

1. Start early, move slowly, and rest at midday in hot weather.
2. Loosen the girth at rest stops but do not remove the saddle while hot.
3. Water frequently and offer feed at planned intervals.
4. Inspect feet, back, and load at every stop.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Animals kick, bite, and crush. Work calmly and never stand
  directly behind an animal.
- A shifted load can cause a fall on steep ground. Stop and rebalance at once.
- Heat stress and exhaustion can kill. Reduce loads in heat and provide shade.
- Treat animal wounds promptly; an infected back sore can end the season.
