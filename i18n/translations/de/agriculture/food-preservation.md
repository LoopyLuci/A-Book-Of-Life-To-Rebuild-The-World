## Overview

Preservation extends harvest into winter. Methods: drying, salting, smoking,
canning, fermenting, and root cellaring.

## Prerequisites

- [Making Fire](../../../../domains/survival/making-fire.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Drying rack
- Salt
- Glass jars with lids
- Smokehouse
- Root cellar

## Procedure

### Sun Drying

1. Slice food 3–6 mm thick.
2. Lay on a rack in full sun, covered with mesh.
3. Turn daily; dry 2–5 days until leathery.
4. Rehydrate in water before use.

### Salting

1. Layer food with coarse salt (1 kg salt per 4 kg food).
2. Weight down; keep submerged in brine.
3. Ready in 2–6 weeks.
4. Rinse before eating.

### Smoking

1. Build a small fire in a smokehouse.
2. Use hardwood (hickory, oak, apple). Never resinous softwoods.
3. Cold smoke (20–30°C) for 1–3 days for preservation.
4. Hot smoke (60–80°C) for immediate consumption.

### Water-Bath Canning (High-Acid Foods)

1. Sterilize jars in boiling water.
2. Fill with hot food, leaving 1 cm headspace.
3. Seal; process in boiling water for the recipe time.
4. Cool; check seal (lid should not flex).

### Pressure Canning (Low-Acid Foods)

1. Use a pressure canner at 10 PSI (115°C) for low-acid foods (meat, beans).
2. Water-bath canning does **not** kill botulism spores.

### Fermenting

1. Submerge vegetables in 2% brine.
2. Keep anaerobic; weight down.
3. Ferment 1–4 weeks at 18–22°C.
4. Refrigerate when sour to taste.

### Root Cellaring

1. Ideal: 0–4°C, 90–95% humidity, dark.
2. Store root vegetables in sand or straw.
3. Ventilate to prevent ethylene buildup.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Botulism from improperly canned low-acid foods is often fatal.
- Discard any can with bulging lid, off-smell, or cloudiness.
- Boil all home-canned low-acid food for 10 minutes before eating.
