# Monocrystalline Silicon Growth

> **Domain:** silicon | **Difficulty:** master | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

The Czochralski pull grows one single crystal from a seed dipped in a
molten melt, and it is how nearly every silicon wafer begins. Temperature,
pull rate, and rotation decide the diameter, the defect density, and the
carrier lifetime of the ingot. The key principle: every disturbance the
crystal feels during the pull is written into it for life.

## Prerequisites

- Silicon Purification, with polysilicon chunks on hand
- A crystal puller with its own heater, seed and crucible handling, and
  inert gas system
- Working knowledge of crystal axes and miller indices
- Vacuum and gas handling practice, and a nitrogen glove box

## Materials & Tools

- Polysilicon chunks, 5 to 25 kg, of semiconductor grade
- A fused silica crucible, 25 to 32 cm diameter, or a quartz lined
  graphite holder
- A puller with a DC heater of 200 to 400 kVA for a production unit
- Seed crystals, 6 to 10 mm diameter, correctly oriented
- An argon or helium purge system with flow control
- A dopant source, such as a quartz boat of boron or phosphorus
- A crystal diameter monitor, edge detection or weight based
- A cropping grinder, a wire saw, and a defect inspection light
- A dry transfer vessel and a nitrogen glove box

## Procedure

### Prepare the Melt

1. Weigh the polysilicon charge for the intended ingot length and
   diameter, and load the crucible and the seed under nitrogen, keeping
   oxygen out completely.
2. Heat the charge until the melt pool forms, close to 1,420 degrees C,
   and degas the melt with a low flow of purge gas.
3. Set the seed temperature so the melt edge stays a few degrees above
   the seed temperature, because that difference sets the shoulder
   diameter.

### Pull the Ingot

1. Dip the seed to just touch the melt and wait for it to melt back, then
   touch again and withdraw slowly to start the shoulder and set the
   diameter.
2. Grow the body of the ingot at a steady pull rate of 0.5 to 2.0 mm per
   minute, and rotate the seed at 0.2 to 0.6 revolutions per second
   throughout.
3. Adjust the heater to hold the melt between 1,415 and 1,425 degrees C,
   keep the melt level at least 10 mm above the crucible lip, grow the
   tail slowly, and then shut off the heater.

### Cool and Inspect

1. Let the ingot cool to below 200 degrees C in the puller over at least
   8 hours, then weigh it and compare the yield against the charge.
2. Crop the tail and the shoulder with the grinder, and check for
   dislocations with a standard rotation etch inspection.
3. Map the resistivity along the ingot to confirm dopant uniformity, and
   record the seed, the charge mass, the pull log, and the defect count.

### Prepare for Wafering

1. Orient the ingot with the flat or with a crystal axis finder.
2. Saw mother wafers with a wire saw at 350 to 500 micrometres.
3. Label each wafer with the ingot number and its position in the ingot,
   and keep the wafers in a sealed carrier until the polish step.

## Safety Notes

- Pullers run at very high current and temperature; work on them only with
  appropriate training, supervision, and equipment.
- Molten silicon at 1,420 degrees C causes severe burns; wear a face
  shield and heat resistant gloves.
- Inert gas systems can asphyxiate; fit oxygen monitors and forced
  ventilation at the puller.
- Handling hydrogen near hot metal risks an explosion; bond and purge the
  gas lines properly.
- The melt and the crystal sit at high voltage; use insulated tooling and
  verify the ground before touching.
- A failed pull can release stored energy and shatter tooling, so never
  stand in the line of fire.
