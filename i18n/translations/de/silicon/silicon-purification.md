# Industrial Silicon Purification

> **Domain:** silicon | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Almost every silicon product starts as quartz and ends as near-pure
element, then as polysilicon for semiconductors. Each stage removes a
class of impurity, and the later stages are progressively harder. The key
principle: the purity of the final material is set by the last
contamination event, not by the first good step.

## Prerequisites

- Metal Smelting, for furnace and slag handling
- Glass Making, for crucible and furnace lining practice
- A working high temperature furnace with proper gas handling

## Materials & Tools

- High purity quartz, SiO2, with under 100 ppm total metal impurities
- Metallurgical grade silicon as feedstock
- A three phase submerged arc furnace, 30 to 60 kVA for a small unit
- Graphite electrodes, graphite crucibles, and carbon crucibles
- Carbon black or petroleum coke as reductant
- Hydrochloric acid for chlorosilane production
- A fractional distillation column in 316L stainless steel
- Vacuum pumps, gas lines, scrubbers, and leak detectors
- Ingot moulds, a ladle, and a lead pot

## Procedure

### Reduce Quartz to Metallurgical Silicon

1. Crush the quartz to 20 to 40 mm, dry it below 0.02 percent moisture,
   and mix it with 25 to 30 percent carbon reductant by mass.
2. Charge the submerged arc furnace and strike the arc, holding 1,800 to
   2,000 degrees C until the silica reduction finishes in 20 to 40
   minutes.
3. Tap the molten silicon at 1,650 to 1,750 degrees C into a ladle, cast
   20 to 40 kg ingots, and cool them for at least 12 hours; expect 98 to
   99 percent silicon with boron and phosphorus still present.

### Upgrade to Polysilicon

1. Hydrochlorinate the metallurgical silicon with HCl at 300 degrees C to
   make trichlorosilane, then purify it by fractional distillation and
   keep the middle cut.
2. Pass the purified silane over a bed of silicon seed at 1,100 degrees C,
   and grow the polysilicon rods on the seed to 150 to 250 mm diameter.
3. Cut the rods into chunks of 5 to 25 kg for the crystal puller, and
   hold boron under 0.1 ppb and total metals under 1 ppb for semiconductor
   feed.

### Control Contamination

1. Clean every vessel with high purity reagents before any grade change,
   and use dedicated tools rather than bare hands.
2. Store the polysilicon under dry nitrogen or argon, and reject material
   that has been out of dry storage for more than 24 hours.
3. Run a blank on every lot, check it for oxygen and carbon by infrared
   analysis, and quarantine any lot that fails the blank.

### Verify the Product

1. Check the resistivity of a test ingot, which reflects the boron and
   phosphorus level.
2. Run an oxygen and carbon analysis on a sample of every lot, and confirm
   that the chunk surface is clean and free of oxide crust.
3. Record the batch, the analysis results, and the storage conditions, and
   release the lot only after the numbers are on the record.

## Safety Notes

- Electric arc furnaces run at lethal voltages and extreme heat; work on
  them only with appropriate training, supervision, and equipment.
- Hydrofluoric acid dissolves bone and can kill from a small exposure; it
  demands specific training, calcium gluconate gel on site, and a written
  emergency plan.
- Trichlorosilane and hydrogen chloride are toxic and corrosive; use them
  only in a scrubbed and ventilated enclosure.
- Silane and hydrogen mixtures are flammable and can detonate; ground and
  vent all equipment.
- Carbon monoxide from the furnace gas must be monitored and scrubbed
  before release.
- Molten silicon and molten carbon sit at 1,700 degrees C; use proper
  refractory tools and never handle hot slag by hand.
