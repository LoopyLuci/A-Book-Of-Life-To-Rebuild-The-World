## Overview

Combustion is the foundational energy conversion. Understanding the fire
triangle (fuel, oxygen, heat) enables efficient stoves, kilns, and furnaces.

## Prerequisites

- [Making Fire](../../../../domains/survival/making-fire.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Fuel (wood, charcoal, peat, dung)
- Firebox or stove
- Chimney or flue
- Air control

## Procedure

### The Fire Triangle

- Remove any leg → fire goes out.
- Control air → control heat.

### Wood Combustion Stages

1. **Drying** (<150°C): moisture evaporates.
2. **Pyrolysis** (150–300°C): volatiles release as smoke.
3. **Gas combustion** (300–600°C): flames consume volatiles.
4. **Char combustion** (600–1,000°C): glowing coals burn.

### Efficient Wood Stove

1. Firebox: 30 × 30 × 40 cm.
2. Air inlet at base with adjustable damper.
3. Baffle plate above fire to increase gas residence time.
4. Chimney: 4–6 m tall, insulated, with a cap.

### Rocket Stove (High Efficiency)

1. Build an L-shaped combustion chamber (J-tube).
2. Vertical riser: insulated, 3× cross-section of feed tube.
3. Heat exchanger at the top.
4. Fuel: small sticks, continuously fed.

### Charcoal Forge/Furnace

1. Charcoal burns at 1,200°C+ with forced air.
2. Use for blacksmithing, smelting, glass.
3. Bellows: bag, box, or centrifugal.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Carbon monoxide from incomplete combustion kills.
  Always vent.
- Chimney fires: clean flues monthly.
- Never burn treated wood (arsenic, chromium).
- Keep a fire extinguisher or sand bucket nearby.
