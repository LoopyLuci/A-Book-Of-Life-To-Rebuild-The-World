# Freshwater Ecology and Water Quality

> **Domain:** biology | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Fresh water carries a food web that runs from algae and bacteria up to
fish, birds, and people. Water quality sets the ceiling on that food web,
and dissolved oxygen is usually the first thing to fail. The key principle:
cold, well-oxygenated water with low nutrient input sustains far more life
than warm, stagnant, enriched water.

## Prerequisites

- Water Quality Testing
- A working knowledge of local rivers, lakes, and their seasonal cycles
- Basic arithmetic with concentrations and units

## Materials & Tools

- Dissolved oxygen meter, or a Winkler titration kit with a burette
- A thermometer reading to 0.1 degrees C
- A pH meter, or a colour comparison kit
- A Secchi disk of 30 to 40 cm on a marked line
- One litre sample bottles with tight caps, and a cooler
- A turbidity tube and a conductivity meter
- A kick net, sorting trays, and a magnifier
- Nitrate and phosphate field test kits
- A field data sheet, one row per station and date

## Procedure

### Survey the Site

1. Fix each sampling station with a marker and a permanent code, and
   record its coordinates.
2. Sample upstream, at the site itself, and downstream of it.
3. Work the same stations in the same season every year, and record the
   time, the weather, and recent rainfall at every visit.

### Measure Water Quality

1. Fill the dissolved oxygen bottle with no trapped air, because a single
   bubble biases the reading low.
2. Measure temperature at the same depth every time, read dissolved
   oxygen in mg/L, and correct the reading for temperature.
3. Measure pH in the field, then conductivity and turbidity, run nitrate
   and phosphate tests on a filtered, chilled sample, and record clarity
   with a Secchi disk.

### Sample the Food Web

1. Kick the bottom substrate upstream of the net at each station.
2. Sort the catch and count the organisms by group, then count the algae
   and rooted plants in a 0.1 square metre quadrat.
3. Note pollution signs such as foam, odour, algal mats, and dead fish,
   then walk upstream to find the source of the loading.

### Interpret and Report

1. Compare each station with the reference station upstream of it.
2. Flag dissolved oxygen below 5 mg/L as a stress signal; saturated water
   at 20 degrees C holds only about 9 mg/L.
3. Flag nitrate above 10 mg/L as nutrient enrichment, plot every
   parameter against date, and publish the sheet each year, including the
   results that showed no change.

## Safety Notes

- Work from stable banks and wear a buoyancy aid; cold water and current
  pull people in without warning.
- Do not wade in deep or fast channels, and avoid flooded channels after
  rain.
- Handle wastewater samples with gloves and wash hands before eating.
- Keep sample bottles clean; a dirty bottle ruins a trace nutrient result.
- Never swim downstream of a suspected sewage or chemical discharge.
- Cover cuts and any skin abrasions, because standing water can carry
  leptospirosis and other pathogens.
