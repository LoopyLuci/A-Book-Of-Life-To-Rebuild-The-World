# Marine Ecology and Fisheries

## Overview

The productivity of the sea rests on a short food chain, so every catch
removes biomass that has to regrow before it can be taken again. Knowing
the trophic levels, the age structure of the catch, and the size of the
nursery grounds turns fishing from taking into managing. The key
principle: harvest only the fraction of the stock the population can
replace in a year.

## Prerequisites

- A local fish identification guide and knowledge of the food web
- A catch recording system and a scale that works in the wet
- Tide tables and a chart of the fishing grounds

## Materials & Tools

- A local fish identification guide, in waterproof form
- A measuring board or callipers reading to 1 mm
- A spring balance or scale reading to 100 g
- A cast net, seine, or hand line appropriate to the fishery
- Numbered tags and a tag applicator for marking releases
- Waterproof data sheets and a pencil
- Temperature, salinity, and pH meters
- Bait, ice, and insulated boxes for the catch
- A ruler, knife, and a sampling tray for gut and scale samples

## Procedure

### Assess the Stock

1. Sample the same grounds and the same months every year, and repeat the
   sample as close as possible to the same date each year.
2. Measure each fish to the nearest millimetre and weigh it to the nearest
   100 g, then take scale or otolith samples from a sample of the catch.
3. Build an age-length table, find the age at first maturity, record the
   proportion of large old spawners left in the catch, and compare catch
   per unit of effort between years and between grounds.

### Read the Ecosystem

1. Measure bottom temperature and salinity at each station, and note
   plankton blooms, water clarity, and the depth of the thermocline.
2. Record the seabed habitat as rock, sand, kelp, or mud, and map the
   nursery grounds, especially the seagrass and mangrove edges.
3. Record spawner aggregations, seabird concentrations, whale sightings,
   jellyfish swarms, algal blooms, and dead fish events.

### Set and Enforce Catch Limits

1. Use the age-length table to estimate the biomass that is still growing,
   and hold the annual catch below the replacement rate of the stock.
2. Close the nursery grounds, protect the spawning season of each
   species, and enforce a minimum size that matches the age at first
   maturity.
3. Use mesh large enough that undersized fish escape the gear, and record
   every landing, including discards, in the community log.

### Rebuild a Depleted Stock

1. Reduce the catch below the replacement rate for several seasons.
2. Protect the spawning aggregations and the nursery grounds, and stop the
   destructive bottom gear.
3. Watch the age-length table for a return of older, larger fish, and raise
   the catch limit only after the stock has rebuilt measurably.

## Safety Notes

- Never fish alone; carry a life jacket and a working radio or phone.
- Cold water shock and drowning kill quickly, so wear the jacket in calm
  weather too.
- Beware of rip currents when wading over reef or sand flats, and know the
  escape route before you enter.
- Use a dehooker and gloves for large fish; never handle a shark bare
  handed.
- Watch for red tide and paralytic shellfish poisoning; do not eat
  shellfish from a closed area.
- Secure the catch in a cool box on ice; spoiled fish carries a real
  health risk.
