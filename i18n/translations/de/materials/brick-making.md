# Brick Making

## Overview

Bricks are clay fired to hardness. They are the backbone of durable
construction — houses, kilns, furnaces, chimneys.

- [Making Fire](../../../../domains/survival/making-fire.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Clay (test: rolls into a smooth snake without cracking)
- Sand (temper)
- Water
- Wooden molds
- Kiln or clamp

## Procedure

### Clay Preparation

1. Dig clay; remove rocks and roots.
2. Add sand (10–20% by volume) to reduce shrinkage.
3. Add water; knead to plastic consistency.
4. Let rest 24 hours (aging improves workability).

### Molding

1. Wet mold; dust with sand.
2. Press clay firmly into mold.
3. Strike off excess with a board.
4. Turn out onto a drying board.

### Drying

1. Air-dry in shade 1–2 weeks.
2. Turn daily to prevent warping.
3. Bone-dry before firing (no moisture).

### Firing

1. Build a clamp: stack bricks with gaps for fire.
2. Cover with mud; leave air channels at base.
3. Fire slowly for 24 hours; peak at 900–1,000°C.
4. Cool 2 days before opening.

### Testing

- Fired brick rings when struck.
- Underfired brick is dull and crumbles.
- Overfired brick is glassy and vitrified (good for kilns).

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Silica dust in clay — wear mask when dry mixing.
- Firing clamp releases smoke and CO — keep away from dwellings.
- Do not use bricks from unknown sources for food-contact surfaces.
