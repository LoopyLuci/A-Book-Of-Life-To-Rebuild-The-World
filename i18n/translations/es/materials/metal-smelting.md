# Metal Smelting

## Overview

Smelting extracts metal from ore using heat and a reducing agent (charcoal).
Start with copper (1,085°C), then bronze (alloy), then iron (1,538°C).

## Prerequisites

- [Charcoal Production](../../../../domains/materials/charcoal-production.md)
- [Simple Tools](../../../../domains/survival/simple-tools.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Ore (malachite for copper; hematite/magnetite for iron)
- Charcoal
- Clay furnace or bloomery
- Bellows
- Hammer and anvil

## Procedure

### Finding Ore

- **Copper:** green-stained rocks (malachite, azurite).
- **Iron:** red-brown rocks (hematite), magnetic black sand (magnetite).
- **Lead:** galena (gray cubic crystals).
- **Tin:** cassiterite (heavy black grains in stream beds).

### Building a Furnace

1. Build a clay cylinder 50 cm tall, 30 cm diameter.
2. Add a tuyere (air pipe) at the base for bellows.
3. Line with clay; let dry slowly.

### Copper Smelting

1. Crush ore to 1 cm pieces.
2. Layer charcoal and ore in furnace.
3. Light; pump bellows continuously.
4. After 45 min, tap slag; collect molten copper at base.
5. Yield: 5–20% copper by ore weight.

### Iron Bloomery

1. Same furnace, taller (1 m).
2. Alternate charcoal and crushed iron ore.
3. Pump bellows for 4–6 hours.
4. Extract a "bloom" — spongy iron mixed with slag.
5. Hammer the bloom while hot to expel slag (wrought iron).

### Bronze (Alloy)

1. Melt copper (1,085°C).
2. Add tin (10–12% by weight).
3. Cast into molds. Bronze is harder than copper and easier to cast.

### Cast Iron

1. Higher carbon content (2–4%).
2. Requires higher temp (1,200°C+) and continuous blast.
3. Brittle but hard; cast into shapes.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Molten metal causes severe burns. Wear leather and eye protection.
- Metal fumes (lead, zinc, arsenic) are toxic — ventilate.
- Never pour molten metal onto wet surfaces (steam explosion).
- Carbon monoxide from charcoal — work outdoors.
