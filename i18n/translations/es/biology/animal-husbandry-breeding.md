# Animal Husbandry and Breeding Systems

## Overview

Keeping livestock means managing breeding, feeding, health, and records so
that the herd works for the household. Genetics sets what an animal can do;
the environment decides how much of that it shows. The key principle: what
you measure, you can select for, and what you do not record, you cannot
improve.

## Prerequisites

- Basic animal care and safe handling experience
- A breeding stock and a herd book or spreadsheet
- A feed table for the species and the local forage
- A relationship with a veterinarian who will visit the farm

## Materials & Tools

- Breeding animals of the chosen breed or cross
- Scales or a weight tape for recording live weight
- Marking tags, ear notches, or a tattoo kit
- Pedigree and performance ledger
- Handling pens, a race, and a safe squeeze chute for larger stock
- Feed, forage, and mineral supplements matched to the species

## Procedure

### Defining the Goal

1. List the traits that matter, such as live weight gain, litter size,
   milk yield, meat yield, or docility.
2. Rank them by the value they add and set a measurement for each one.
3. Establish the flock or herd average before selecting anything, so that
   later improvement can be shown.

### Choosing a Mating System

1. Use purebred mating to fix a known set of traits, and expect the
   inbreeding coefficient to rise by about 1 percent per generation in a
   closed population.
2. Use crossbreeding where hybrid vigour matters; first crosses commonly
   gain 3 to 5 percent in growth over the parent breeds.
3. Backcross the best offspring to the parent breed to keep most of the
   improved genetics, replacing about half the genome each generation.
4. Avoid mating close relatives, and bring in new breeding stock from
   outside the herd every few years.

### Recording and Selecting

1. Record every birth with both parent identifications, the date, and the
   number born.
2. Weigh animals at a fixed age, such as weaning and yearling, so the
   comparison is fair.
3. Estimate heritability from your own records; milk yield in cattle sits
    near 0.3, while litter size in pigs sits near 0.1 to 0.15.
4. Keep only the animals that meet the standard as parents, and cull
    animals with repeated structural faults, poor udders, or chronic
    lameness.
5. Expect the response to selection to be about the heritability times the
    selection differential, check the real gain each year, and plan mating
    calendars around the species: about 283 days of gestation in cattle,
    114 days in pigs, and 147 to 152 days in sheep.

## Safety Notes

- Large animals can crush, kick, and trample; work from a protected
  position, keep the escape route clear, and never stand directly behind an
  animal.
- Handling a boar, a bull, or a mare with a foal is high risk; use
  barriers and experienced handlers.
- Several animal diseases cross to people, including ringworm, tetanus,
  brucellosis, and rabies; wash hands, wear gloves for calving, and keep
  vaccination records current.
- Feed that has moulded or been stored damp can cause mycotoxicosis; never
  feed material that smells sour or shows fuzz.
- Keep young children out of animal pens, and never leave a child alone
  with livestock of any size.
- Use a proper handling facility rather than improvising with rope and
  gates, and wear boots that can be washed and disinfected.
