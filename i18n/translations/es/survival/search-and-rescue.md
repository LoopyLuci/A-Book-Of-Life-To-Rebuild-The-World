# Search and Rescue

> **Domain:** survival | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Most lost-person searches are won by fast, organized coverage of likely places,
not by large numbers of people walking at random. A small team can run a hasty
search, mark clues, and hand a clean map to professional responders. The
priority is to find the person, keep searchers safe, and avoid destroying
tracks or evidence.

## Prerequisites

- Basic first aid
- Rope and knot skills
- A map, compass, and radio

## Materials & Tools

- Topographic maps and compass or GPS
- Whistle, signal mirror, and high-visibility vests
- Headlamps and spare batteries
- First aid kit and patient packaging
- Flagging tape, numbered clue markers, and notebook
- Radio or mobile phones with offline maps
- Water, food, and weather protection

## Procedure

### 1. Establish command and information

1. Appoint one search leader and one safety officer; everyone reports to them.
2. Record the person's name, age, clothing, medical needs, mobility, and last
   known point with time.
3. Ask what they were doing, who saw them last, and whether they have a phone.
4. Define the search area and a clear turnaround time before anyone leaves.

### 2. Run a hasty search

1. Check the most likely places first: trails, water, roads, vehicles, and
   buildings the person knows.
2. Send small teams along likely routes with radios and a check-in schedule.
3. Call the person's name and stop to listen; use whistles in a repeated
   three-blast pattern.
4. Mark any clue, footprint, or personal item and leave it in place.

### 3. Expand to grid search

1. Assign parallel lanes spaced so searchers can see the ground between them.
2. Walk slowly and look under, behind, and above eye level.
3. Record the area covered, direction, and time on the map.
4. Re-search likely routes at night with lights; people often move downhill or
   toward water.

### 4. Stabilize and extract

1. Once found, assess airway, breathing, circulation, and temperature before
   moving the person.
2. Treat hypothermia, dehydration, and injuries; do not give food or drink to
   an unresponsive person.
3. Package the patient and move only if the scene is unsafe or extraction is
   practical.
4. Hand over the patient, clues, and search map to responding authorities.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Searches involve water, cliffs, weather, and unstable ground.
  Untrained volunteers must not enter these areas.
- Do not search alone or leave the assigned area without telling the leader.
- Treat every clue as evidence; do not pick up or disturb personal items.
- Stop and reassess when searchers become tired, cold, or dark closes in.
