# Waterborne Disease

> **Domain:** health | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Waterborne disease usually appears as acute watery diarrhoea, vomiting, fever,
or jaundice. Most deaths are preventable with oral rehydration, continued
feeding, and zinc for children. Antibiotics are reserved for specific syndromes
such as cholera or dysentery, and the source must be fixed or the outbreak will
continue.

## Prerequisites

- Basic first aid and infection control
- Access to safe water and sanitation

## Materials & Tools

- WHO-formulated oral rehydration salts (ORS)
- Clean water and a measuring container
- Zinc tablets or dispersible tablets
- Soap and handwashing station
- Thermometer
- Oral rehydration solution chart
- Referral forms and transport plan
- Water testing and treatment supplies

## Procedure

### 1. Assess dehydration

1. Check consciousness, thirst, skin pinch, sunken eyes, and ability to drink.
2. Classify as no dehydration, some dehydration, or severe dehydration using a
   standard chart.
3. Weigh children and record urine output if possible.
4. Look for blood in stool, persistent fever, or jaundice, which change the
   treatment plan.

### 2. Rehydrate

1. Give ORS in small frequent sips; use a cup and spoon for children.
2. Replace ongoing losses after each loose stool.
3. Continue breastfeeding and normal food; do not stop feeding.
4. For severe dehydration, start intravenous fluids only if trained and
   equipped; otherwise refer urgently while giving ORS.

### 3. Treat and refer

1. Give zinc for 10-14 days to children with diarrhoea, following national
   dosing guidance.
2. Use antibiotics only for suspected cholera, dysentery, or another confirmed
   indication; do not treat every diarrhoea case.
3. Refer for blood in stool, severe dehydration, persistent vomiting, or
   altered consciousness.
4. Isolate patients from the water supply and provide a safe latrine.

### 4. Stop the source

1. Test and treat drinking water; boil or disinfect until tests are clear.
2. Separate drinking water from washing water and protect wells and springs.
3. Clean latrines, handwashing points, and food preparation surfaces.
4. Record cases by date and location to detect an outbreak early.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Cholera and severe diarrhoea can kill a child within hours.
  Start rehydration immediately and refer early.
- Do not give sugary drinks, undiluted juice, or unclean water as rehydration.
- Health workers must use gloves and wash hands after contact with stool.
- Untreated water and open defecation keep the outbreak alive even when
  patients recover.
