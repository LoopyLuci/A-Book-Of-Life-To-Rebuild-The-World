# Water Quality Testing

> **Domain:** water | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Field testing tells you whether water needs treatment now; it cannot prove that
water is safe. A clear sample can still carry viruses, and a chlorine test can
miss chemical contamination. Use simple tests to screen for turbidity, residual
disinfectant, and gross contamination, then treat drinking water by boiling,
filtration plus disinfection, or another verified method.

## Prerequisites

- A protected or treated water source
- Basic understanding of dilution and test kit instructions

## Materials & Tools

- Clean sample bottles and a clean collecting cup
- Turbidity tube or clear measuring cylinder
- Chlorine test strips or DPD colorimeter
- pH strips or meter
- Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) presence/absence vials
- Thermometer
- Gloves and eye protection
- Notebook and labels

## Procedure

### 1. Collect a representative sample

1. Label the bottle with source, date, time, and collector.
2. Flush the tap or pump for 1-2 minutes, or collect from a flowing spring.
3. Rinse the bottle with sample water, then fill without touching the inside.
4. Test chlorine and pH immediately; refrigerate microbiological samples if
   they must be transported.

### 2. Measure physical indicators

1. Read turbidity against the tube scale or a meter; record the value in NTU.
2. Note color, odor, and visible particles. A musty or chemical smell is a
   reason to find another source, not just to disinfect.
3. Measure temperature; warm water holds less oxygen and can grow bacteria
   faster.
4. Record the source level after heavy rain, when contamination is highest.

### 3. Test disinfection and gross contamination

1. Follow the kit instructions exactly for chlorine residual; record free and
   total chlorine if available.
2. A faint chlorine residual after treatment suggests the dose was adequate;
   zero residual means the water may be unprotected.
3. Use H2S vials as a screening test: a black result indicates possible
   bacterial contamination and requires treatment and repeat testing.
4. Test pH; most disinfection works best between 6.5 and 8.5.

### 4. Interpret and act

1. Treat all surface water and any sample with visible turbidity before
   drinking.
2. If tests are ambiguous, boil or use a verified filter and disinfectant.
3. Repeat tests after treatment and after any change in source or plumbing.
4. Keep a log so you can spot a source that is getting worse.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Test reagents can irritate skin and eyes. Wear gloves and
  eye protection and keep kits away from children.
- Never assume a clear sample is safe; field kits do not detect all pathogens
  or chemicals.
- Do not drink water just because it has no smell or because it comes from a
  deep well.
- Samples from floods, mining areas, or industrial sites need laboratory
  testing for metals and organic chemicals.
