# Efficient Lighting

> **Domain:** energy | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Lighting is often the first electrical load a household can afford, and the
easiest to reduce. Daylight, white walls, task lamps, and efficient LEDs can cut
demand by most of the load while improving safety and study conditions. Fuel
lamps are a last resort because they produce indoor air pollution and fire risk.

## Prerequisites

- A safe power source or charged battery
- Basic wiring and fixture installation

## Materials & Tools

- LED bulbs or DC lamps with known wattage
- Lamp holders and switched sockets
- Reflectors or light-coloured paint
- Extension cable rated for the load
- Fuses or breakers sized for the circuit
- Cleaning cloth and ladder
- Light meter or phone app for comparison

## Procedure

### 1. Use daylight first

1. Open curtains and remove obstructions from windows.
2. Paint ceilings and walls a light colour to reflect light deeper into rooms.
3. Place work surfaces near windows and use mirrors or light shelves where
   useful.
4. Keep windows clean; dirty glazing can cut light significantly.

### 2. Choose efficient lamps

1. Compare lumens per watt, not just wattage; LEDs typically deliver far more
   light per watt than incandescent bulbs.
2. Match colour temperature to the task: cool white for work, warmer light for
   evenings.
3. Use DC lamps on DC circuits to avoid inverter losses.
4. Buy fixtures with replaceable bulbs and standard fittings where possible.

### 3. Install safely

1. Switch off and isolate the circuit before working.
2. Use correct polarity and fuses; keep connections dry and enclosed.
3. Mount fixtures securely and keep cables away from walkways and water.
4. Provide task lighting instead of lighting an entire room brightly.

### 4. Maintain and measure

1. Clean lamps and shades; dust can reduce output noticeably.
2. Replace flickering or dimming LEDs promptly; driver failure can overheat.
3. Track hours and energy use with a plug-in meter or daily log.
4. Compare illuminance at the work surface before and after changes.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Kerosene, candle, and unvented gas lamps release carbon
  monoxide and fine particles. Do not use them indoors for long periods.
- Cheap LED drivers can fail dangerously; use certified products and enclose
  connections.
- Ladders and roof work cause falls; use a spotter and keep three points of
  contact.
- Do not overload extension leads or daisy-chain power strips.
