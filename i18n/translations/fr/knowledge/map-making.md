# Map Making

> **Domain:** knowledge | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

A useful map records what a traveler needs: distance, direction, water, hazards,
and landmarks. You do not need a survey-grade instrument. A compass, a measured
baseline, pacing, and a consistent symbol set can produce a sketch map accurate
enough for route planning, disaster response, and local records.

## Prerequisites

- Compass and pace-count skills
- Basic arithmetic and scale drawing

## Materials & Tools

- Compass with adjustable declination
- Measuring tape or rope for a baseline
- Graph paper, clipboard, pencil, and eraser
- GPS or phone for checkpoints
- Range poles or flags
- Clinometer or phone level for slopes
- Waterproof map case

## Procedure

### 1. Set the scale and orientation

1. Decide the area and the smallest feature to show; choose a scale such as
   1:2,000 for a village or 1:50,000 for a district.
2. Draw a north arrow and note magnetic declination.
3. Mark a known baseline on the ground and measure it twice.
4. Establish two fixed reference points that will not move.

### 2. Survey by bearing and distance

1. From each reference point, take bearings to corners, buildings, wells, and
   road junctions.
2. Measure or pace each distance; calibrate your pace over 100 m first.
3. Plot each point by drawing a line along its bearing and marking the scaled
   distance.
4. Use triangulation from two known points to locate features you cannot reach.

### 3. Add terrain and symbols

1. Sketch contours from slope breaks and spot heights, or use a GPS track.
2. Mark water, seasonal streams, flood lines, bridges, and steep grades.
3. Use a legend with consistent symbols for buildings, fields, roads, and
   hazards.
4. Add a title, date, scale bar, north arrow, and surveyor's name.

### 4. Check and reproduce

1. Walk the map with a local guide and correct errors in the field.
2. Compare distances and bearings at several checkpoints.
3. Copy the final map in ink or scan it; store a master away from the field
   copy.
4. Record magnetic declination and the date, since it changes slowly.

## Safety Notes

- **Green hazard:** Field mapping is low risk, but traffic, weather, and
  trespassing are real concerns. Ask permission and avoid restricted areas.
- Do not rely on a phone battery alone; carry a compass and paper map.
- River crossings and steep ground require a partner and a turn-around time.
- A sketch map is not a legal property survey.
