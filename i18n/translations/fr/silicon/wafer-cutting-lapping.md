# Wafer Cutting, Lapping and Polishing

> **Domain:** silicon | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Ingots become wafers through grinding, wire sawing, lapping, and a final
chemical-mechanical polish. Every stage removes the damage left by the
one before it. The key principle: the saw damage must be fully removed
by lapping before polishing starts, or the finished surface carries
cracks into the device.

## Prerequisites

- Monocrystalline Silicon Growth, with a grown ingot available
- Abrasive machining practice and safe use of rotating equipment
- Cleanroom discipline for handling finished wafers

## Materials & Tools

- An ingot grinder with a diamond wheel, 100 to 200 grit
- A wire saw with a 40 to 60 micrometre diamond wire
- A lapping plate and conditioner with alumina slurry in 1, 3, 9, and
  27 micrometre grades
- A polishing machine with a porous pad and a chemical mechanical slurry
- Deionised water, NMP or isopropanol for cleaning
- A vacuum chuck and chucking wax
- An edge profiler, or a small angle diamond tool, for edge rounding
- A wafer thickness gauge and a flatness tester
- A heated chuck held at 60 to 80 degrees C for polishing
- A microscope for inspecting the edge and the final surface

## Procedure

### Grind and Orient the Ingot

1. Grind the ingot to the target diameter, leaving 0.5 mm of stock for the
   final cut.
2. Mark the crystal orientation, either a notch or a flat, and confirm it
   on an X-ray or laser orientation finder.
3. Grind the end face square so the ingot will chuck cleanly.

### Saw the Wafers

1. Mount the ingot on the wire saw with the wire running parallel to the
   crystal axis.
2. Set the wire speed to 0.6 to 1.0 metres per second, keep the feed
   rate slow, hold the slurry below 25 degrees C, and keep the wire
   tension even.
3. Cut wafers at 350 to 500 micrometres, allowing for a 200 micrometre
   kerf, and rinse each wafer in deionised water as soon as it leaves the
   saw.

### Ledge the Wafer

1. Inspect the edge under magnification for chips and wire marks.
2. Lap the edge at 30 to 45 degrees to remove the sharp corner.
3. Probe the edge thickness along the full circle; a 300 micrometre edge
   is a common target, and one chipped edge ruins a later step.

### Polish and Verify

1. Mount the wafer on the polishing chuck with vacuum, condition the pad,
   and polish with the finest slurry while holding the chuck at 60 to 80
   degrees C.
2. Remove the wafer, rinse it in deionised water, and measure the removal;
   a good polish takes off 0.5 to 1.0 micrometres.
3. Check that thickness variation stays under 10 micrometres and bow and
   warp under 30 micrometres on a 200 mm wafer, inspect the surface for
   scratches and residue, and count the surface particles.

## Safety Notes

- Wire saws and grinders throw broken wire and abrasive grit; guard the
  machine and wear eye protection.
- Grinding wheels can shatter, so never exceed the rated speed and always
  use a guard.
- Slurries and cleaning solvents are irritants; ventilate the area and
  wear gloves.
- Wafer edges cut deeply and bleed through thin gloves; handle wafers
  with a vacuum tool.
- Alumina dust is a respiratory hazard, so wet the work and wear a mask.
- NMP and isopropanol are flammable; keep them away from hot equipment
  and sparks.
