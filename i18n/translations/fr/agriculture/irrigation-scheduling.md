# Irrigation Scheduling

> **Domain:** agriculture | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Irrigation scheduling replaces habit with measurement: water when the root zone
is approaching its limit, apply enough to refill it, and stop before runoff.
Overwatering wastes fuel and nutrients and can drown roots; underwatering
reduces yield even when leaves look healthy. A moisture test, a known application
rate, and a simple log are enough to improve most small plots.

## Prerequisites

- A reliable water source and storage
- Basic soil and crop knowledge

## Materials & Tools

- Soil auger or digging trowel
- Moisture meter or tensiometers
- Catch cans or graduated containers
- Flow meter, timer, or calibrated bucket
- Drip tape, emitters, or furrow gates
- Mulch
- Notebook and rain gauge

## Procedure

### 1. Measure the soil and the application rate

1. Dig 15-30 cm down and squeeze a handful of soil; crumbly and slightly damp
   is workable, while dry dust or free water indicates a problem.
2. Use a moisture meter or tensiometer at root depth and record readings at the
   same time each day.
3. Measure how long the system takes to deliver 10 mm of water using catch cans
   or a flow meter.
4. Map the plot into zones with similar soil, slope, and crop stage.

### 2. Set a water budget

1. Estimate crop water use from local evapotranspiration data or a reference
   crop chart; adjust for sun, wind, and crop stage.
2. Subtract effective rainfall recorded by the rain gauge.
3. Apply water to refill the root zone, not to wet the surface every day.
4. Keep a simple ledger of date, zone, millimetres applied, and rainfall.

### 3. Apply water efficiently

1. Irrigate early morning or evening to reduce evaporation and wind loss.
2. Use drip or furrow systems that deliver water to roots rather than leaves.
3. Alternate wet and dry cycles where crops allow; this discourages some
   diseases and saves water.
4. Stop when water begins to run off or when the root zone is refilled.

### 4. Maintain and adjust

1. Flush drip lines and clean filters weekly; check emitters for clogging.
2. Repair leaks and reset timers after power or pressure changes.
3. Mulch beds to slow evaporation and keep soil temperature stable.
4. Recheck the schedule after thinning, harvest, or a change in weather.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Wet soil, ponds, and irrigation ditches are drowning and
  fall hazards, especially for children.
- Do not irrigate with untreated sewage or industrial water on edible crops.
- Slippery paths and buried pipes can cause falls and injuries.
- Pumps and electrical controls must be grounded and kept out of floodwater.
