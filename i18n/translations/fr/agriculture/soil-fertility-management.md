# Soil Fertility Management

> **Domain:** agriculture | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Fertility is not just nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. It is organic matter,
soil structure, pH, and a living community of roots and microbes. Feed the soil
with compost, cover crops, and balanced minerals, and most crops need fewer
external inputs. Test before adding amendments; guessing at lime or manure rates
can create deficiencies, salinity, or polluted water.

## Prerequisites

- Composting or access to mature compost
- Basic crop rotation knowledge

## Materials & Tools

- Soil test results or a field test kit
- Mature compost or well-rotted manure
- Cover crop seed
- Agricultural lime or gypsum only if indicated
- Mulch and erosion-control fabric
- Spade, hoe, and wheelbarrow
- Soil auger and sample bags

## Procedure

### 1. Sample and assess

1. Divide the plot into zones with similar soil, slope, and cropping history.
2. Take 10-15 cores from each zone at 0-20 cm depth, mix them, and send a
   composite sample to a laboratory.
3. Record pH, organic matter, phosphorus, potassium, and any micronutrients
   flagged by the lab.
4. Note drainage, compaction, erosion, and earthworm activity in a field log.

### 2. Build organic matter

1. Apply 2-5 cm of mature compost to beds and incorporate it shallowly.
2. Grow cover crops in the off-season: legumes for nitrogen, grasses for
   biomass, and deep-rooted species for compaction.
3. Keep soil covered with mulch or living plants to protect structure and
   moisture.
4. Avoid burning crop residues; compost or incorporate them instead.

### 3. Correct pH and minerals

1. Apply lime only when the test calls for it; over-liming locks up
   micronutrients.
2. Use gypsum for sodic or compacted soils only after confirming the problem.
3. Add phosphorus and potassium according to crop removal and soil test levels.
4. Re-test after one or two seasons rather than adding more blindly.

### 4. Rotate and protect

1. Rotate plant families to interrupt pests and diseases and balance nutrient
   demand.
2. Include a legume at least every third season where climate allows.
3. Keep rows across the slope, maintain grass waterways, and avoid working wet
   soil.
4. Record yields and amendments so the next season can adjust the plan.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Manure and compost can carry pathogens. Use well-rotted
  material on food crops and wash produce.
- Lime and fertilizer dust irritate eyes and lungs; wear gloves, eye protection,
  and a dust mask.
- Do not spread manure or fertilizer before heavy rain or near wells and
  streams.
- Soil tests from contaminated sites need laboratory analysis for heavy metals.
