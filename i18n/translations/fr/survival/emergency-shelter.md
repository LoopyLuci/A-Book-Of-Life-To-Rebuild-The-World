# Emergency Shelter

> **Domain:** survival | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Exposure kills faster than hunger or thirst. A shelter must address four
threats: wind, rain, cold, and ground conduction. Priority order: insulate
from the ground, block wind, shed rain, retain heat.

## Prerequisites

- None

## Materials & Tools

- Debris (branches, leaves, grass)
- Cordage (see [Rope & Cordage](../../../../domains/survival/rope-cordage.md))
- Knife or sharp stone
- Location with natural windbreak (rock, tree, ridge)

## Procedure

### Lean-To

1. Find a ridgepole (3–4 m straight branch).
2. Anchor one end in a tree fork or rock; support the other with a Y-stick.
3. Lean branches against the ridgepole at 45° to form a wall.
4. Cover with leaves, moss, or bark. Layer thickly — 30 cm minimum.
5. Build a fire reflector wall of logs 1 m from the opening.

### Debris Hut

1. Build an A-frame with two forked sticks and a ridgepole.
2. Pile leaves and branches to 1 m thickness on both sides.
3. Fill the interior with dry leaves for insulation.
4. Crawl in feet-first; plug the entrance with a leaf bundle.

### Snow Cave (Winter)

1. Find a drift at least 2 m deep.
2. Dig a tunnel 1 m long, then up into a dome.
3. Smooth the ceiling to prevent dripping.
4. Poke a ventilation hole with a ski pole.
5. Build a sleeping platform 30 cm above the floor.

## Safety Notes

- Never sleep directly on the ground — conduction steals heat 25x faster than air.
- Snow caves require ventilation; CO₂ builds up while sleeping.
- Avoid dead trees overhead (widowmakers).
