# Soap Making

> **Domain:** survival | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources

source of information.*

## Overview

Soap prevents disease. It is made by saponifying fat with lye. The reaction
produces soap and glycerin.

## Prerequisites

- [Making Fire](../../../../domains/survival/making-fire.md)
- [Charcoal Production](../../../../domains/materials/charcoal-production.md) (for lye)

## Materials & Tools

- Fat (tallow, lard, or vegetable oil)
- Hardwood ash
- Rainwater (soft)
- Wooden or ceramic pot (never aluminum)
- Wooden spoon
- Mold (wooden box)

## Procedure

### Making Lye

1. Fill a wooden barrel with hardwood ash.
2. Slowly pour rainwater through the ash.
3. Collect the dark liquid that drips out — this is lye (potassium hydroxide).
4. Test: a fresh egg or potato should float with a coin-sized area above the surface.

### Cold-Process Soap

1. Weigh 500 g fat; melt in a pot.
2. Slowly add lye solution (about 200 mL) while stirring.
3. Stir until "trace" — the mixture leaves a visible trail on the surface.
4. Pour into a wooden mold lined with cloth.
5. Cover and insulate for 24 hours.
6. Unmold, cut into bars, cure 4–6 weeks.

### Hot-Process (Faster)

1. After trace, simmer the mixture for 3 hours.
2. It will gel, then liquefy.
3. Pour into molds; cure 1 week.

## Safety Notes

- **Lye burns skin and eyes.** Wear gloves and eye protection.
- Always add lye **to** water, never water to lye (violent reaction).
- Never use aluminum or cast iron — lye corrodes them.
- Cure soap fully; uncured soap is caustic.
