# Wood Gasification

> **Domain:** energy | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Gasification converts wood into combustible gas (CO + H₂ + CH₄) that can
power engines, generators, and stoves.

## Prerequisites

- [Charcoal Production](../../../../domains/materials/charcoal-production.md)
- [Fire & Combustion](../../../../domains/energy/fire-combustion.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Gasifier (downdraft or updraft)
- Wood chips or pellets
- Pipes (steel)
- Filter (cyclone + fabric)
- Cooler

## Procedure

### Downdraft Gasifier

1. Build a steel reactor 60 cm tall, 20 cm diameter.
2. Air inlet at top.
3. Throat (constriction) at ⅓ height.
4. Combustion zone: 900–1,100°C.
5. Reduction zone below: 700–900°C produces CO and H₂.

### Feedstock

- Wood chips 2–5 cm, moisture <20%
- Avoid bark and leaves (tar and ash).
- Pelletize fines for uniform feed.

### Gas Cleaning

1. **Cyclone:** removes large particles.
2. **Cooler:** drops temp to <50°C.
3. **Fabric filter:** removes remaining particles.
4. **Optional scrubber:** water or oil for tar.

### Using the Gas

- **Generator:** modify spark-ignition engine (petrol) with gas mixer.
- **Stove:** direct burn with secondary air.
- **Boiler:** fire into combustion chamber.

### Efficiency

- 1 kg wood → 2.5–3 Nm³ gas → ~5 MJ energy.
- 20–25% of wood energy becomes usable gas.
- Char and heat are byproducts.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Producer gas is 20–30% CO — highly toxic, odorless.
  Never operate indoors. Use CO detectors.
- Gas is explosive in air (12–75% range). No sparks.
- Tar clogs pipes and engines; maintain cleaning schedule.
- Do not inhale gas while lighting; use a torch on a stick.
