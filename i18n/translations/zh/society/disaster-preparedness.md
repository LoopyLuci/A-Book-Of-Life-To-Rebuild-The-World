# Disaster Preparedness

> **Domain:** society | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Preparedness is a cycle: understand the hazards, reduce the risks, plan the
response, practice it, and update it. A community plan works when people know
their roles, shelters are reachable, and supplies are stored where they can be
found. A plan written by outsiders and never practiced is worse than no plan
because it creates false confidence.

## Prerequisites

- A map of the community and surrounding hazards
- Agreement on who leads and how decisions are made

## Materials & Tools

- Hazard and resource map
- Contact list and radio plan
- Emergency supply caches
- Shelter signs and route markers
- First aid and triage kits
- Registration and family reunification forms
- Drill log and after-action notes

## Procedure

### 1. Map hazards and resources

1. Mark flood zones, landslide slopes, fire risk, industrial sites, and safe
   high ground.
2. Locate water, food stores, clinics, shelters, and alternative routes.
3. List vulnerable people: older adults, people with disabilities, pregnant
   women, children, and those without transport.
4. Identify likely secondary hazards such as contaminated water or downed
   power lines.

### 2. Assign roles and thresholds

1. Name a coordinator, deputy, and section leads for shelter, health, logistics,
   and communication.
2. Agree on trigger points for warning, evacuation, and shelter opening.
3. Set a communication tree and a fallback meeting place.
4. Write the plan in plain language and translate it into local languages.

### 3. Prepare supplies and shelters

1. Stock water, food, first aid, lighting, tools, and sanitation for at least
   three days.
2. Inspect shelters for access, ventilation, water, and structural safety.
3. Store supplies in multiple locations and rotate them before expiry.
4. Mark routes and shelters so they are visible at night.

### 4. Drill and improve

1. Run a tabletop exercise, then a short field drill.
2. Time the warning, evacuation, and shelter setup; note bottlenecks.
3. Ask participants what failed and assign fixes with dates.
4. Review the plan yearly and after every real event.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Drills can cause traffic, falls, and panic. Tell
  participants what is happening and keep routes clear.
- Do not create false alarms; coordinate with local authorities.
- Shelters need separate sanitation, safe water, and protection from violence.
- People with disabilities and animals need explicit transport and access plans.
