# Plant Propagation and Grafting

> **Domain:** biology | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Propagation multiplies a plant exactly, and grafting combines the roots of
one plant with the top of another. Seed gives new varieties, cuttings give
copies, and grafting gives a better root system or an earlier crop. The key
principle: every graft needs live cambium touching live cambium, and every
cutting must stay moist until it has rooted.

## Prerequisites

- Plant physiology and plant cell basics
- A rooting area where humidity can be held
- A sharp knife and grafting tools in good order

## Materials & Tools

- Mother plants of the varieties to propagate
- Sharp grafting knife, secateurs, and grafting tape
- Rooting medium: perlite, coarse sand, or a 1:1 perlite and peat mix
- Seed trays, pots, and a humidity dome or clear cover
- Rooting hormone powder for hard cuttings
- Labels, a pencil, and a propagation record sheet

## Procedure

### Raising from Seed

1. Test seed viability by soaking 10 seeds in water; seeds that sink are
   usually the viable ones.
2. Break dormancy where it is needed, by holding moist seed at 1 to 5
   degrees C for 2 to 12 weeks.
3. Sow at a depth of about three times the seed width and keep the medium
   at 20 to 25 degrees C.
4. Move the seedlings into full light as soon as the first true leaves
   appear.

### Rooting from Cuttings

1. Take hardwood cuttings from dormant wood, semi-hardwood cuttings from
   partly ripened shoots, and softwood cuttings from the tips of active
   growth.
2. Cut a clean 10 to 15 cm shoot with a sharp blade just below a node, and
   strip the lower leaves.
3. Dip the base in rooting hormone and insert it two thirds deep into the
   moist medium.
4. Enclose the tray in a humidity dome and hold it at 20 to 25 degrees C out
   of direct sun.
5. Check for roots after 3 to 6 weeks by tugging a cutting gently; pot it
   up when it resists the pull.

### Grafting and Budding

1. Graft in late winter or early spring, while the stock is dormant and the
    sap has not started to move, and make a clean 3 to 5 cm cut on both the
    stock and the scion with a full face of cambium.
2. Use a whip-and-tongue graft when the diameters match, and a splice or
    bark graft when the scion is smaller, then wrap the union tightly and
    coat it with grafting wax.
3. Check the graft after 2 to 3 weeks, remove rootstock suckers as they
    appear, and cut back above the union once the graft is growing
    strongly.

## Safety Notes

- Cutting tools cause deep cuts; use a sharp blade, cut away from your
  body, and keep your fingers behind the edge.
- Sap from many plants is irritating or toxic; wear gloves and wash after
  handling cuttings.
- Bitumen-based grafting wax sticks to skin and can burn when it warms in
  sunlight; apply it in the shade and wash it off with warm soapy water.
- Keep rooting hormone powders sealed and labelled, and store them away
  from kitchen stores and food crops.
- Work in a ventilated space when using horticultural solvents or resin
  grafting tapes.
- Clean tools between plants so that viruses and bacteria are not carried
  from one stock to the next.
