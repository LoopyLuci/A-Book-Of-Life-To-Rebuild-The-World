# Teaching Literacy

> **Domain:** knowledge | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Literacy teaching works best as a short, regular routine: hear sounds, connect
them to letters, blend words, read a short text, and write a sentence. Learners
need practice at the right level, not a lecture about grammar. A community
teacher can run a small group with a chalkboard, paper, and a simple sequence.

## Prerequisites

- Fluency in the language of instruction
- A quiet space and regular meeting time

## Materials & Tools

- Chalkboard or large paper
- Letter cards and word cards
- Decodable readers or short local texts
- Notebooks and pencils
- Assessment sheet
- Picture cards for vocabulary
- Timer or bell

## Procedure

### 1. Build oral language and sounds

1. Start with familiar words, names, and local stories.
2. Clap syllables and isolate first and last sounds.
3. Teach one or two letter-sound pairs at a time; review previous ones daily.
4. Use pictures and actions to anchor new vocabulary.

### 2. Connect sounds to print

1. Show the letter, say its sound, and have learners trace it in sand or on
   paper.
2. Blend sounds into words: /m/ /a/ /p/ becomes "map".
3. Build words with letter cards and change one sound at a time.
4. Read the word aloud, then have each learner read it individually.

### 3. Read and write a short text

1. Use a decodable text that contains only taught sounds and a few new words.
2. Read together, then in pairs, then silently.
3. Ask two comprehension questions: who, what, where, or why.
4. Finish with a dictated sentence or a personal sentence using a new word.

### 4. Assess and regroup

1. Check letter sounds, word reading, and a short writing sample every few weeks.
2. Group learners by the skill they need, not by age.
3. Give extra practice to those who are stuck; do not move on too quickly.
4. Celebrate progress and keep a simple record for each learner.

## Safety Notes

- **Green hazard:** Teaching is low risk, but classrooms still need safe exits,
  clean water, and protection from weather.
- Never use corporal punishment or public humiliation; they drive learners away.
- Adapt examples to the local language and culture.
- Protect learner records and privacy.
