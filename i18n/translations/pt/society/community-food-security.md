# Community Food Security Planning

> **Domain:** society | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Food security is not only how much a community grows; it is whether every
household can get enough safe, nutritious food through the year. A plan maps
production, stores, gaps, and vulnerable households, then sets reserves and
distribution rules before a crisis. The target is at least three months of
staple reserves where storage allows.

## Prerequisites

- Community organizing
- Basic record keeping

## Materials & Tools

- Household food survey forms
- Maps and records
- Scales and calculators
- Storage inventory sheets
- Seed stock
- Distribution lists and a notice board

## Procedure

### 1. Assess the situation

1. Survey households for stores, income, garden capacity, and dietary needs.
2. Map local production, wild foods, markets, and storage.
3. Identify vulnerable groups: children, pregnant women, elders, disabled
   people, and households without land.
4. Estimate the lean season and the size of the gap.

### 2. Set reserves and priorities

1. Agree a target reserve, such as three months of staples per household.
2. Prioritise diverse, storable foods rather than a single crop.
3. Set clear eligibility and distribution rules before reserves are needed.
4. Protect seed stock separately from eating stock.

### 3. Build and store

1. Support household and communal storage that is dry, cool, and pest-proof.
2. Promote gardens, small livestock, and food preservation.
3. Organise shared tools, threshing, and storage to reduce costs.
4. Record all stock movements and check for spoilage monthly.

### 4. Distribute and review

1. Distribute through trusted local institutions with public records.
2. Target the most vulnerable first; avoid giving to the loudest voices.
3. Review the plan before planting and before winter.
4. Adjust for failed harvests, price changes, and new arrivals.

## Safety Notes

- **Green hazard:** The main risks are social, not physical. Unequal access and
  corruption can cause malnutrition even when food exists.
- Never distribute mouldy or expired food; it can cause acute poisoning.
- Keep distribution peaceful and transparent; publish the rules and amounts.
- Respect dignity and privacy when identifying vulnerable households.
