# Redox Chemistry and Electrochemical Cells

> **Domain:** chemistry | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Redox reactions drive corrosion, batteries, metal refining, and every form
of chemical energy storage. A galvanic cell turns a chemical reaction into
current; an electrolytic cell does the reverse. The key principle is that
electrons are conserved, so a balanced half-reaction pairs always move the
same number of electrons.

## Materials & Tools

- Zinc and copper strips, plus copper(II) sulfate solution
- Two 250 mL beakers, a salt bridge of soaked cloth or agar, and a wire
- A multimeter or voltmeter, alligator leads, and crocodile clips
- A bench DC power supply for the electrolytic cell
- Sandpaper, a ruler, and a 1 M sodium chloride solution

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Balance Redox Equations

1. Split the reaction into an oxidation half and a reduction half.
2. Balance every atom except oxygen and hydrogen.
3. Add water on one side and hydrogen ions on the other.
4. Balance hydrogen with hydrogen ions, then charge with electrons.
5. Multiply the half-reactions so the electron counts match, then add.

### Stage 2: Read the Standard Potentials

1. Look up the standard reduction potential for each half-reaction.
2. The cathode is the half-cell with the more positive value.
3. Subtract to get the cell potential, E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode.
4. Count the transferred electrons, n, for later energy calculations.
5. Note that the standard state is 25 °C, 1 M, and 101.325 kPa.

### Stage 3: Build a Galvanic Cell

1. Polish the electrodes and rinse them in distilled water.
2. Fill one beaker with zinc in zinc sulfate and the other with copper in
   copper sulfate.
3. Install the salt bridge so the two solutions stay separate.
4. Connect the electrodes to the voltmeter, not to each other.
5. Record the voltage, then reverse the leads and check the sign flips.

### Stage 4: Build an Electrolytic Cell

1. Use an external DC source with a series resistor of about 100 ohms.
2. Make the workpiece the cathode, the positive terminal, and the
   sacrificial metal the anode, the negative terminal.
3. Use graphite or platinum electrodes for inert reactions.
4. Hold the current density near 10 mA per square centimetre.
5. Stop when the deposited mass stops increasing over two checks.

### Reference Values

1. Zinc: Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ gives Zn, E° = -0.76 V.
2. Copper: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ gives Cu, E° = +0.34 V.
3. A zinc-copper cell therefore has E°cell = 1.10 V.
4. The Faraday constant is 96485 coulombs per mole of electrons.
5. Free energy follows ΔG° = -nFE°, so 212 kJ per mole for that cell.

## Safety Notes

- Metal salt solutions are harmful; wear goggles and gloves.
- Hydrogen gas forms at the cathode and is flammable; keep it away from
  flames and ventilate the bench.
- Never short a cell by wiring the electrodes together directly.
- Grid electrolysis only with appropriate training, supervision, and
  equipment, because chlorine gas can form from chloride solutions.
- Wear insulated gloves when handling a power supply or hot electrolyte.
