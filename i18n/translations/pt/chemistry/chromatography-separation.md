# Chromatographic Separation

> **Domain:** chemistry | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Chromatography separates a mixture by letting a mobile phase carry the
components at different speeds through a stationary phase. It is the
routine way to check that a synthesis worked and the standard way to purify
a product. The key principle is that like dissolves like, so each component
is held back in proportion to how much it prefers the stationary phase.

## Prerequisites

- Organic Functional Groups
- Solvent Extraction

## Materials & Tools

- Silica gel TLC plates, 0.1 to 0.25 mm layer, 20 by 20 cm
- Capillary tubes or a fine pipette for spotting
- A developing chamber or wide-mouth jar with a tight lid
- Petroleum ether (hexane, 60 to 80 °C) and ethyl acetate as eluent
- A glass column, 1 cm bore and 20 cm long, with a stopcock
- Silica gel 60 to 250 microns, sand, and collection vials
- A 254 nm UV lamp, or an iodine chamber or potassium permanganate stain

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Choose the Eluent

1. Begin with 100 percent petroleum ether for a mostly non-polar mixture.
2. Add ethyl acetate 5 percent at a time until the spots move.
3. Aim for an Rf of 0.3 to 0.7 for the least polar component of interest.
4. Raise solvent polarity when a compound sticks to the baseline.
5. Lower it when the compound runs with the solvent front.

### Stage 2: Run a Thin Layer Plate

1. Draw a pencil line 1 cm above the base of the plate.
2. Spot 1 to 2 mm drops of sample on the line and dry between each spot.
3. Place the plate in a chamber saturated with solvent, base down.
4. Let the solvent rise about 5 cm, then take the plate out.
5. Mark the solvent front while it is still wet.
6. Develop the spots under 254 nm UV or with a stain.
7. Measure each spot distance and divide by the front distance for Rf.

### Stage 3: Run a Column

1. Plug the column base with cotton, then pour in silica and bed it down.
2. Add a second cotton plug on top of the silica.
3. Flush the column with eluent until the packing settles.
4. Load the sample as a narrow band on top of the column.
5. Elute with the solvent chosen in Stage 1, then increase polarity.
6. Collect 2 to 5 mL fractions and test each one by TLC.
7. Combine the matching fractions and strip off the solvent.

### Stage 4: Read the Result

1. Compute Rf as spot distance over solvent front distance.
2. Expect the Rf of a compound to rise slightly on a thinner layer, since
   the solvent front travels further.
3. Run a standard alongside the unknowns to confirm identification.
4. In a column, the same compound comes off in a fixed volume range.
5. In high performance liquid chromatography, read the retention time
   instead and keep the flow rate constant.

## Safety Notes

- Petroleum ether, ethyl acetate, and methanol are flammable; work away
  from flames and sparks and keep the chamber lid on.
- Solvent vapours are toxic and heavier than air; work in a fume hood and
  ventilate the bench.
- Chlorinated solvents such as dichloromethane are carcinogenic; use them
  only with appropriate training, supervision, and equipment.
- UV lamps emit harmful radiation; keep the skin covered and never look
  at the lamp directly.
- Wear nitrile gloves and eye protection; change gloves between solvents.
- Collect all solvent waste for recovery or proper disposal.
