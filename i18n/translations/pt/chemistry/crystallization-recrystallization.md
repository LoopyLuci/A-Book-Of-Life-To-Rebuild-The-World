# Crystallization and Recrystallization

> **Domain:** chemistry | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** green

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Crystallization purifies a solid by rebuilding it as a clean lattice from a
hot saturated solution, and recrystallization repeats the cycle to raise purity
further. It is the cheapest way to upgrade crude salt, alum, or a metal salt to
reagent grade. The key principle is that solubility usually rises with
temperature, so dissolve the impure solid hot and cool it until only the
target crystallises.

## Prerequisites

- Basic handling of glassware and a heat source
- A balance that reads to 0.01 g
- A data sheet for the target substance giving its solubility curve

## Materials & Tools

- Crude salt, alum, or another solid to purify
- Solvent in which the target is hot-soluble and cold-insoluble
- Beaker, glass stirring rod, and a heat-resistant dish
- Funnel, filter paper or cloth, and a clean receiving vessel
- Ice bath and a thermometer reading to 1 °C
- Colander or funnel for draining, plus a filter paper cone for drying
- Balance, label cards, and a logbook

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Choose the solvent

1. Pick a solvent in which the target is sparingly soluble when cold and
   dissolves readily when hot, such as water for table salt or ethanol for
   benzoic acid.
2. Check that the solvent does not react with the compound, and note the
   solubility at 20 °C and at boiling.

### Stage 2: Dissolve the crude solid

1. Weigh the crude material into a beaker and add solvent in small portions
   while stirring.
2. Heat to 80-90 °C and add the last drops of solvent until nothing more
   dissolves.

### Stage 3: Remove insoluble impurities

1. Pour the hot solution through a pre-warmed filter into a clean vessel.
2. Rinse the residue on the filter with a little hot solvent and discard it.

### Stage 4: Cool and crystallise

1. Cool the clear solution slowly to room temperature, undisturbed, so the
   crystals grow large and well formed.
2. Place the vessel in an ice bath at 0 °C for 20 minutes to finish the crop.
3. Scrape down crystals stuck to the wall with a clean glass rod.

### Stage 5: Collect, wash and dry

1. Decant or drain the crystals and rinse them with a few millilitres of
   cold solvent to remove the mother liquor.
2. Dry in a filter paper cone or over a desiccant, not over direct heat.
3. Repeat the whole cycle once on the dried crop; two passes usually reach
   reagent grade. Store dry in a labelled, closed vial.

## Safety Notes

- Never taste a crystal to test it; check identity with a melting point or an
  indicator paper instead.
- Use a heat-resistant dish and a heat source rated for it; hot solution
  cracks cold glass.
- Filter hot liquid carefully, since a hot saturated solution flashes steam and
  spits.
- Keep flammable solvents such as ethanol and acetone away from flame and
  sparks; use a water bath or low heat for them.
- Let the vessel cool before handling it, because glass stays hot well after
  the flame is out.
- Label every container with the solute, the solvent, and the date.
