## Overview

Natural dyes color textiles, leather, and paper. Most require a mordant
(metal salt) to fix the color to fiber.

## Prerequisites

- [Rope & Cordage](../../../../domains/survival/rope-cordage.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Plant materials (see table)
- Mordant: alum, iron (rusty nails), copper, tannin
- Large pot (enamel or stainless — never aluminum for mordanting)
- Heat source

## Procedure

### Common Dye Plants

| Color | Source |
|-------|--------|
| Yellow | Onion skin, turmeric, marigold, chamomile |
| Orange | Carrot tops, safflower |
| Red | Madder root, cochineal (insect), beet (poor fastness) |
| Blue | Indigo, woad |
| Green | Overdye yellow with indigo |
| Brown | Walnut hulls, tea, coffee |
| Black | Oak gall + iron mordant |
| Purple | Logwood + iron, elderberry |

### Mordanting

1. Dissolve 10% alum (by fiber weight) in hot water.
2. Soak clean fiber 1 hour at 80°C.
3. Cool; rinse.

### Dyeing

1. Chop plant material; simmer 1 hour.
2. Strain; add wet, mordanted fiber.
3. Simmer 1 hour; stir gently.
4. Cool overnight in the dye bath.
5. Rinse until water runs clear.

### Modifying Color

- Add iron (rusty nails) → darkens, "saddens" colors.
- Add copper → shifts greens.
- Add vinegar → brightens reds.
- Add wood ash → shifts blues.

### Indigo (Vat Dyeing)

1. Ferment indigo with urine or lye + fructose.
2. Dip fiber; it appears yellow-green in the vat.
3. Expose to air; it oxidizes to blue.
4. Repeat dips for deeper blue.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Mordants are metal salts — some (chromium, tin) are toxic.
  Use alum, iron, or copper only.
- Never use aluminum pots for dyeing or mordanting.
- Ventilate when simmering dye plants (some are allergenic).
- Some plants (pokeweed, bloodroot) are toxic; do not handle without gloves.
