# Cargo Lashing

> **Domain:** transport | **Difficulty:** novice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

A load that shifts can overturn a cart, injure an animal, or throw a driver.
Lashing is a system: distribute weight, lower the centre of gravity, attach to
strong points, and check the tension again after a few kilometres. More straps
do not fix a bad load.

## Prerequisites

- Basic knots and rope handling
- Familiarity with the cart, vehicle, or animal harness

## Materials & Tools

- Rated straps, chains, or rope
- Soft shackles or edge protectors
- Chocks and anti-slip matting
- Tarpaulin and tie-down points
- Gloves and eye protection
- Inspection log and knife

## Procedure

### 1. Plan the load

1. Read the vehicle, cart, or animal load limit and axle limits.
2. Put the heaviest items low and over the axle, not behind it.
3. Balance left and right and keep the load as narrow as practical.
4. Protect sharp edges and separate incompatible goods.

### 2. Secure the load

1. Use at least two independent restraints for a heavy load.
2. Attach straps to rated frame points, not to body panels or fuel lines.
3. Tighten evenly and use edge protectors where a strap crosses a sharp corner.
4. Use a knot that will not jam, and tie off loose ends.

### 3. Check and travel

1. Stop after the first few kilometres and re-tension straps; loads settle.
2. Recheck at every stop, after rough ground, and after rain.
3. Watch for leaning, smell of fuel, or unusual steering before continuing.
4. Unload with a spotter and chock the wheels or hobble the animal.

### 4. Inspect and store

1. Retire straps with cuts, UV damage, or broken fibres.
2. Coil rope dry and store it out of sunlight.
3. Record damage and replace hardware before the next trip.
4. Keep a knife accessible for a quick release in an emergency.

## Safety Notes

- **Yellow hazard:** Shifting loads can crush limbs and overturn vehicles.
  Keep people clear while tensioning and unloading.
- Never use damaged straps, rusty chains, or knots that can slip.
- Do not exceed the weakest component's working load limit.
- Fuel, chemicals, and animals need separate, ventilated securement.
