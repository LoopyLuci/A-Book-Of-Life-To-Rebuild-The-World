# Immunology and Vaccination

> **Domain:** biology | **Difficulty:** apprentice | **Safety:** yellow

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Immunity has two layers: innate defence, which acts within minutes, and
adaptive defence, which is slower but remembers. Vaccination trains the
adaptive layer so the body meets a pathogen faster and milder. The key
principle: a vaccine that has broken its cold chain is not a weaker
vaccine, it is no vaccine at all.

## Prerequisites

- A clear immunisation register for the community and the children in it
- A cold chain with a written temperature log
- A named person trained to recognise and respond to anaphylaxis
- A referral route to a clinic for cases beyond field capability

## Materials & Tools

- Vaccine vials with batch number, expiry date, and vial monitor
- Insulated cold chain carrier with conditioned coolant packs
- A temperature monitor or minimum/maximum thermometer for the carrier
- Auto-disable syringes in the sizes the schedule requires
- Diluent matched to each vaccine that needs it
- Alcohol handrub, sterile swabs, and gloves
- A sharps container and a sealed clinical waste bag
- Adverse event forms and a batch register
- A timer and a quiet waiting area

## Procedure

### Store and Transport

1. Keep most refrigerated vaccines between +2 degrees C and +8 degrees C,
   and never freeze one, because freezing destroys several of them.
2. Condition the coolant pack until it is cold but not frozen so vials
   never sit against ice, and keep the carrier closed and shaded.
3. Record the storage temperature twice a day and at every transfer, and
   check the vaccine vial monitor at each opening.

### Plan Coverage

1. List the diseases in the national or regional schedule, and map the
   schedule onto the birth cohort and the catch-up cohort.
2. Trace the register to find the children who were missed, and schedule
   sessions on market days and school days so families can attend.
3. Book the second dose against the interval given in the schedule, and
   plan a surge session after any missed round.

### Run the Session

1. Check the expiry date, the batch number, and the cold chain log before
   the first dose of the day, and screen each person for contraindications
   using the national screening questions.
2. Reconstitute the vaccine only with the diluent named on the label, draw
   the dose with a sterile syringe, one per person, and give it by the
   route on the label into the correct site.
3. Record the batch number, the date, the site, and the name of the
   vaccinator, watch each person for 15 minutes, and put the used syringe
   straight into the sharps container.

### Respond to Adverse Events

1. Keep the person lying down and do not leave them alone.
2. Treat anaphylaxis with adrenaline only if you are trained and following
   the national protocol, and send for medical help at once.
3. Report the event to the surveillance system within 24 hours, keep the
   vial and the record so the batch can be traced, and tell the supplier
   if other cases share that batch.

## Safety Notes

- Giving the wrong product, the wrong route, or the wrong dose is the most
  common preventable harm in a session; read every label aloud.
- Use a new sterile needle and syringe for every dose; never reuse one.
- A broken cold chain makes a vaccine useless without looking wrong, so
  log the temperature every time.
- A needlestick needs immediate care; do not squeeze or scrub the wound,
  and report it the same day.
- Keep an adrenaline kit and a trained responder present at every session.
- Do not vaccinate during acute severe illness; follow national
  guidance on deferral.
