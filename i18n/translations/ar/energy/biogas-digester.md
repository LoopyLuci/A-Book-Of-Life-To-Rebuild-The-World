# Biogas Digester

> **Domain:** energy | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources

source of information.*

## Overview

Anaerobic digestion of manure, food waste, and crop residue produces biogas
(60% CH₄, 40% CO₂) plus a nutrient-rich effluent for fertilizer.

## Prerequisites

- [Raising Livestock](../../../../domains/agriculture/raising-livestock.md)
- [Fire & Combustion](../../../../domains/energy/fire-combustion.md)

## Materials & Tools

- Digester (fixed-dome, floating-drum, or tubular)
- Feedstock: manure, food scraps
- Water
- Gas storage (bag or drum)
- Stove or generator

## Procedure

### Fixed-Dome (Deenbandhu)

1. Dig a pit 2–3 m diameter, 2 m deep.
2. Build brick or concrete dome over the top with a gas outlet.
3. Inlet pipe at bottom for slurry.
4. Outlet pipe for effluent at ⅔ height.
5. Volume: 1 m³ per cow per day.

### Floating-Drum

1. Dig a cylindrical pit.
2. Place a steel drum inverted over the top — it rises with gas pressure.
3. Gas outlet from drum top.

### Tubular (Flexible)

1. Lay a long plastic or rubber bag in a trench.
2. Fill ⅔ with slurry.
3. Gas collects at the top; use a weighted bag for pressure.

### Feeding

- Cow manure: 1:1 with water
- Pig manure: 1:2 with water
- Food waste: pre-compost, then feed
- C:N ratio: 20–30:1
- pH: 6.8–7.4
- Temperature: 30–40°C (mesophilic)

### Retention Time

- 15–30 days at 35°C.
- 40–60 days at 20°C.

### Using Biogas

- Burn directly in a stove (adjust air for methane).
- 1 m³ biogas ≈ 0.6 L petrol ≈ 6 kWh.
- 1 cow yields ~0.4 m³/day.

## Safety Notes

- **Red hazard:** Biogas is explosive (5–15% in air) and can asphyxiate.
  Never enter a digester pit — H₂S and CO₂ displace oxygen.
- Keep flames away from gas leaks.
- Ventilate any enclosed gas equipment.
- Effluent is caustic; wear gloves.
