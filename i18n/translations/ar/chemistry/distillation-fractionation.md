# Distillation and Fractionation

> **Domain:** chemistry | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

Distillation separates a liquid mixture by boiling point, and fractionation
adds a column so that many components can be resolved. It is the core
operation for recovering solvent, purifying ethanol, and splitting petroleum
fractions. The key principle is that the vapour above a boiling liquid is
enriched in the more volatile component, so repeated condensation and
vaporisation sharpens the separation.

## Prerequisites

- Working knowledge of safe glass handling and of sealing ground joints
- A heat source that can be held at a controlled temperature
- The safety data sheet for every liquid you intend to heat
- Prior training with flammable liquids and with compressed gas

## Materials & Tools

- Round-bottom or flat-bottom flask, 100 to 500 mL
- Liebig condenser with hoses and a water supply
- Fractionating column, Vigreux type or packed with glass beads
- Still head, thermometer adapter, and receiving flask
- Thermometer covering -10 to 200 °C
- Heating mantle or oil bath with a controller, plus boiling chips
- Stand and clamps for every joint
- Ice bath for cooling the receiver
- Suitable fire extinguisher and a spill kit

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Set up the still

1. Assemble the still on a stand over a non-flammable surface, with the
   condenser running downhill so condensate flows to the receiver.
2. Secure every joint with a clamp and keep the heating vessel seated in its
   mantle; never heat a clamped flask that is not supported.
3. Fill the condenser with cold water from the bottom out, check every joint
   for leaks, and add boiling chips or a stir bar before any heat is applied.

### Stage 2: Run a simple distillation

1. Charge the flask to no more than two thirds of its volume and add the
   boiling chips.
2. Heat slowly and set the thermometer bulb level with the sidearm so it
   reads the vapour temperature.
3. Discard the first few millilitres of distillate, then collect the cut at
   the expected boiling point.
4. Stop the heat when the thermometer falls below the boiling point or the
   residue darkens.

### Stage 3: Run a fractional distillation

1. Fit a Vigreux column or a column packed with glass beads, and put a small
   condenser above the still head so the vapour partly refluxes.
2. Apply less heat than in the simple distillation above, and let the column
   establish its temperature gradient.
3. Collect separate cuts into labelled receivers as the temperature steps
   through each boiling plateau.

### Stage 4: Recover and clean up

1. Keep the receiver vented to the atmosphere or to a scrubber, and never
   cap a system that can release vapour.
2. Cool the still before dismantling it and dispose of the residues as the
   safety data sheet directs.

## Safety Notes

- Fire is the dominant hazard: vapour-air mixtures ignite readily. Use no open
  flame, no smoking, and keep a suitable extinguisher within reach.
- Boiling liquid spatters and burns. Never heat a closed or overfilled
  vessel, and let the mantle reach temperature before adding liquid.
- Glass apparatus under vacuum or pressure can implode. Reject cracked glass
  and never seal a hot system.
- Many organic solvents are toxic, flammable, and carcinogenic. Work in a
  fume hood and know the flash point of what you are heating.
- Blocked columns and fouled joints build pressure. Keep a vent path open at
  all times.
- Concentrating an unknown mixture can detonate peroxides. Do not distill
  anything you have not identified.
- Fractionating petroleum fractions floods a room with flammable vapour and
  can produce carbon monoxide. Work outdoors or with forced ventilation.
