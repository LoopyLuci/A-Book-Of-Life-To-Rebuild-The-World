# Marine Diesel Propulsion

> **Domain:** ship | **Difficulty:** journeyman | **Safety:** red

*Reference material only. Verify this information against primary sources
and qualified professionals before acting on it. This entry is not a sole
source of information.*

## Overview

The marine diesel replaced the steam engine because it turns fuel into work
without a boiler, a condenser, or a fire to manage, and because it starts
and changes load faster. The key principle is that a diesel is a heat
engine first and a machine second: its power comes from the expansion of
compressed air, and everything in the fuel system, the timing, and the
cooling exists to keep the cylinder clean and the metal at a temperature
the lubricating oil can survive. Diagnose the heat engine first, then the
machine.

## Prerequisites

- The two-stroke and the four-stroke cycles, and the compression ratio
- Fuel and lubricating oil specifications, and the viscosity grades
- The fuel injection pump, the injector, and the fuel oil system
- Reduction gearing, the clutch, and the propeller shaft
- Fire safety for fuel, and confined space entry

## Materials & Tools

- The engine: the cylinder head, the piston, the rings, the liner, the
  connecting rod, the crankshaft, and the bearings
- The fuel system: the service tank, the day tank, the booster pump, the
  gravity oil tank, the filters, the injection pump, and the injectors
- The lubricating system: the sump, the oil cooler, the filters, and the
  oil mist detector
- The cooling: the sea water pumps, the coolers, and the expansion tank
- The starting gear: the air compressor, the air receiver, and the
  turning gear
- Test instruments: a cylinder pressure gauge, a borescope, a fuel
  analyser, and a handheld tachometer
- Spares: injector tips, rings, filter elements, and the belts

## Procedure

### Choose and prepare the fuel

1. Know the fuel: the ISO 8217 classes from marine gas oil through the
   heavy fuel oils with 3.5 percent and more sulfur, matched to the
   engine's rating and to its injector.
2. Heat and clean the fuel: the viscosity has to be right at the
   injector, and the fuel must be free of water and of cat fines.
3. Test the fuel with the analyser for the water and the sediment, take
   the sample from the bottom of the tank after it has settled, and keep
   the fuel apart: the day tank is the overflow of the service tank, and
   the overflow must return to the service tank and never reach a header
   or a hot surface.

### Run the engine

1. Keep the cylinder pressures within 10 percent of the mean and record
   them every watch; a falling compression on one cylinder shows up as a
   hard start and as heavy smoke.
2. Watch the exhaust temperature; a high reading on one cylinder means a
   fouled or a stuck injector, and an uneven reading means an uneven fuel
   supply.
3. Watch the crankcase pressure and the oil mist detector, where a high
   crankcase pressure means a blow-by that will end as an explosion, and
   watch the cooling: the jacket outlet should be within 10 degrees
   Celsius of the inlet, and the sea inlet strainer must be clean.

### Maintain the fuel and the lubricating systems

1. Change the fuel filters at the maker's interval and clean the
   injectors at the stated running hours, using the maker's method and
   never a wire.
2. Check the lubricating oil's viscosity and its water content every
   watch and keep the oil pressure above the alarm; the bearings fail
   without oil long before they fail with dirty oil.
3. Sample the oil and the filters for wear and read the results as a
   trend, keep the fuel and the lubricating oil tanks segregated and
   their vents clear, and test the level alarms.

### Handle the drive

1. Align the engine, the gearbox, and the shaft; a misalignment destroys
   the thrust bearing and the couplings.
2. Check the torsional critical speed and the engine's order; a resonance
   in the shafting shakes the ship apart.
3. Set the clutch and, on a controllable pitch propeller, the pitch, so
   that the engine is never loaded when the clutch is engaged.
4. Reverse and change speed only at low load, and never engage the clutch
   against a turning screw.

### Troubleshoot

1. A failure to start: check the starting air at 20 to 30 bar, the fuel
   pressure at the injector, which should be 3 to 6 bar above the
   cylinder pressure, the turning gear, and the interlocks.
2. A loss of power: check the fuel supply and the filters, the charge air
   and the turbocharger, and the fouling on the propeller.
3. Smoke tells the story: black for too much fuel or too little air,
   white for water in the fuel or coolant in the cylinder, blue for oil.
4. A hot cylinder: check the water jacket and the liner; a heavy blow-by
   calls for a compression test and for the rings.

## Safety Notes

- The injection system is a hydraulic weapon: the fuel pressure at the
  injector is 1000 to 2000 bar, and an injection into the skin causes a
  severe injury. Never test an injector by hand, only on a proper rig.
- Fuel is a flammable liquid with a flash point near 60 degrees Celsius,
  and its vapour is heavier than air, so it collects in the lowest part of
  the machinery space.
- Fire and explosion are the classic marine casualties: keep the fuel
  system sound, keep the fire detection and the fixed extinguishing
  system in service, and watch the oil mist.
- The exhaust manifold and the turbocharger are at 400 to 500 degrees
  Celsius, and the lagging hides the metal; treat every hot surface as
  hot.
- Carbon monoxide and the diesel exhaust: ventilate the machinery space
  and never run the engine with an air inlet blocked or a funnel closed.
- Stored energy: the air receiver holds 20 to 30 bar, and the injection
  system is still pressurised after the engine stops.
- Guard the coupling and the shafting, and never open a crankcase
  without lockout and tagout.
- During bunkering no smoking and no hot work, and the tank's sounding
  and its scupper are watched from the deck.
