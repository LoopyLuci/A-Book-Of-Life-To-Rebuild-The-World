## 1. Reference material only

The content of this project is published for **reference, education and
research purposes only**. It is a starting point for a reader's own
investigation. It is not a course, a qualification, a work instruction, or an
authoritative specification.

Nothing in this project is a substitute for the primary sources it cites, for
the manufacturer's documentation, for a standard published by a standards body,
or for the judgement of a qualified professional who has seen the actual
equipment, site, material or person in front of them.

## 2. Always double-check the information

**Every procedure, figure, quantity, tolerance, standard number and safety
instruction in this project must be independently verified against the
original source before it is relied upon.** The recommended practice is:

1. Read the cited source, not only this summary of it.
2. Check the entry's **Verification** status and its date. An entry marked
   `draft` is an unverified outline. An entry marked `field-tested` reflects
   one reported field test by one reported tester in one reported place.
3. Confirm that local law, regulation, standard, certification and site rules
   are satisfied. This project does not know your jurisdiction.
4. Have the work checked by someone qualified to sign it off for the risk
   involved, and by someone who can operate the equipment safely.
5. Treat any number, ratio, temperature, pressure, dose, dosage or deadline
   as a starting point to be confirmed, not as a fact to be copied.

## 3. Not a single source of information

**No entry in this project is a complete or sole source of information on its
subject.** A single entry is a summary. A summary omits context, edge cases,
failure modes, regional variation, supplier variation and the reasons behind a
rule. Never plan, approve, treat, build, operate, certify, sail, fly, feed
people, treat animals, or make any other decision on the strength of one
entry, one screenshot, one chat answer or one message.

Where this project disagrees with a primary source, a regulation, a standard,
an equipment manual or a qualified professional, **the primary source and the
professional win.** Where two entries in this project disagree with each
other, treat both as unproven and open an issue.

## 5. Safety

Entries carry a safety level.

| Level | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| `green` | Ordinary care and normal protective equipment. |
| `yellow` | Real hazards exist. Read the safety notes and use appropriate protection and supervision. |
| `red` | Serious risk of injury, death, fire, toxic exposure, contamination or legal liability. These entries describe work that is performed only with appropriate training, supervision, protective equipment, facilities and authorisation. |

A safety level is a summary of the risk as the author understood it. It is not
a risk assessment, and it does not replace one. A `green` entry is not a
guarantee of safety.

Entries about chemistry, electricity, pressure, heavy lifting, working at
height, confined spaces, biological materials, radiation, lasers, cryogenic
liquids, hot work, vessels, machinery and structural work describe work that
is regulated in most places. Following an entry does not make the work legal
or safe. It may make it more dangerous, if the person following it does not
know what they are doing.

**Do not perform a procedure you are not trained and equipped to perform.**
If a procedure seems to ask for more than you have, stop and get help.

## 6. Verification status is not a warranty

The verification lifecycle is `draft` → `peer-reviewed` → `field-tested` →
`deprecated`.

- `draft` means nobody has checked it yet.
- `peer-reviewed` means two contributors read it. It does not mean it was
  tested, and it does not mean a qualified professional approved it.
- `field-tested` means at least one reported tester carried out the procedure
  and reported a result. It does not mean it is safe, correct in every
  setting, or suitable for your situation.

A field test is a report from one place at one time. It is evidence, not
proof. Contributors are not insurers.
